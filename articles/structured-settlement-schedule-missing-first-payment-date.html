Your Structured Settlement Schedule Has No First Payment Date

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Your structured settlement lists monthly payments but leaves the start date blank. Get the actual schedule before budgeting around it. A proposed illustration, signed settlement agreement, assignment and annuity schedule can describe different stages. Find the controlling documents and compare their amounts and timing.

Identify the signed payment obligation

Ask for the signed agreement or release and its payment exhibit, any qualified assignment, the issued annuity schedule if available, and any approval order required in the case. Look for the payee, amount, payment interval, first payment date and any event that starts the schedule. Separate an up-front cash payment from recurring payments and any future lump sums.

For a qualified assignment under federal tax law, 26 U.S.C. § 130 defines conditions including periodic payments fixed and determinable as to amount and timing. The statute also describes the relationship between a qualifying funding asset and the periodic payments. Read the actual agreement and assignment to determine how your schedule is set. 26 U.S.C. § 130

Ask for a written start date from the responsible party

Some records identify an obligor or assignee and an annuity issuer. Record which entity confirms the payment obligation and which handles delivery. New York's structured settlement definitions, in a law governing transfers of payment rights, distinguish the obligor, annuity issuer and documents that describe the arrangement. Your agreement identifies the roles for your case. New York General Obligations Law § 5-1701

In an example, the signed exhibit says the first $750 payment is due January 15, while a preliminary estimate leaves the date blank. Compare the final documents and ask for confirmation that they agree. If the agreement instead ties commencement to funding or approval, request proof of the triggering event and the resulting calendar date. Record when the payment is due and how and when it is expected to arrive.

Use the confirmed schedule for planning

Enter the dates confirmed from your documents in the Insurance Claim Date and Reminder Planner. Keep any missing or conflicting date open for written resolution before relying on it. If a date or incident in the release itself differs from the agreed terms, see how to reconcile the draft release. If the distribution also includes a contingency fee deduction, read how to trace the signed fee terms to each payment.
