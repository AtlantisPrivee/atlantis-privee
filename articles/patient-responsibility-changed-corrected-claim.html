Your Patient Share Changed After the Provider Corrected the Claim

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

The insurer initially assigns you $65 for a visit. The provider corrects the claim, and a new explanation of benefits, or EOB, shows $115. Ask what changed in the submitted claim and what changed when the plan processed it. A new amount can reflect a different code, units, service date or payment decision, depending on the record.

Separate the provider's submission from the insurer's decision

Get the original and corrected EOBs. Ask the billing office for the date it sent the correction, the original reference, the replacement reference if available and a description of the change. Ask the insurer which version it accepted and whether it decided all the affected lines. UnitedHealthcare's administrative guide is one example of a payer instructing providers to submit a corrected claim as a replacement with the original reference. Use your own plan's identifiers and procedure. UnitedHealthcare provider guide on claim corrections

Mark the lines that changed. Compare the service information, allowed amount, plan payment and patient responsibility. Check whether the plan's explanation distinguishes a deductible, copayment, coinsurance or an amount assigned for another reason. CMS explains how the patient amount on an EOB relates to the provider's bill and payments already made. CMS EOB guide

Reconcile what you actually paid

Suppose the old notice assigns $65 and the new active notice assigns $115. You paid $40 after the first bill. If that payment remains credited to the same service and there are no other changes, the current amount still due on that line would be $75. If the provider's new statement shows the full $115, ask where the $40 posted and request an updated ledger.

Use the medical bill and EOB comparison tool to put the active decision beside the payment record. If the corrected submission is still under review, note that status and ask for the expected update. If you disagree with the decision itself, ask the plan for the review or appeal instructions on the current notice.

Keep the earlier payment receipt even if the provider reissued the bill under a new number. Ask the billing office to confirm where the original payment is credited before comparing the revised totals.

If both notices keep the same number, confirm which revision remains active. If the difference is in the deductible, compare the deductible with the bill and plan ledger.
