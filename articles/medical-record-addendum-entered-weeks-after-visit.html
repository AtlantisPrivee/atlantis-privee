A Medical Record Addendum Was Entered Weeks After Your Visit

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A visit note is dated May 4. A paragraph attached to it is dated May 27 and changes a detail about how your injury occurred. Keep both. The date care took place, the date a clinician entered information and the date you downloaded the chart are separate facts.

Request the record that shows the relationship

Ask the provider for the encounter note and related addenda or corrections, with the available author, signature and entry dates. Identify the exact visit and the statement you are trying to reconcile. If a portal view shows only the newest text, ask the records office how it labels an original note and a later entry and what versions it can provide. Preserve the files as received.

CMS's Medicare review manual discusses amendments, corrections and delayed entries. It expects the change to be identifiable and its date and author to be clear. Those instructions apply to Medicare record review. The same fields can help you understand the chronology of an injury chart. CMS Program Integrity Manual, section 3.3.2.5

Put the changed detail next to its source

For example, the initial note says left wrist pain began “yesterday.” The addendum says “three days ago” based on a later conversation. Record what each note says and the date of the conversation the addendum describes, if shown. Ask the provider whether the addendum corrects the original statement, adds a later history or refers to a separate encounter. The way it answers that question can affect how you explain the difference to an insurer.

If you believe the record contains an inaccurate or incomplete statement, ask the provider for its amendment process. HHS explains the HIPAA right to request a change and the option to submit a statement of disagreement if the covered provider or health plan denies the request. Identify the entry and supply any relevant documentation through its approved channel. HHS guidance on medical record corrections

Keep later care changes in their own column

Use the Medical Records Gap Organizer to track the original note, the addendum and any requested clarification. If the insurer has the earlier copy, identify the newer version by date and author so a reviewer can see what changed. For a different kind of later change, see how to update a therapy cost estimate after the plan moves to home exercises. If the disputed date appears on the bill rather than the chart, read how to reconcile repeated service dates after an overnight stay.
