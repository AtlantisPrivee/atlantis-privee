Construction Injuries: Workers’ Compensation and Third-Party Claims

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

One site can have a general contractor, subcontractors, an equipment supplier, and a property owner. Record who employed the injured worker, who directed the task, and who provided or controlled the equipment involved. The workers' compensation question starts with the employment and work injury. A separate civil question may concern the conduct of another entity and what caused the injury. California DWC injured worker guidebook

Keep two records of the same incident

For workers' compensation, document when you reported the injury, the claim form, medical treatment, and restrictions. State rules govern coverage and benefits. For a possible third-party claim, preserve the task assignment, equipment identification, witness accounts, inspection records, and the role of each business. California Labor Code § 3852 is one example of a statute that allows an employee to pursue a claim against a person other than the employer while also claiming workers' compensation, subject to applicable rules. California Supreme Court discussion of Labor Code § 3852 · U.S. Department of Labor state agency directory

Use separate columns for employer, site supervisor, equipment owner, and witness. If a rented lift was involved, record the serial number, who inspected it, and the time of the last service. A service record can help identify what the supplier did. A daily work order may address who controlled the task.

Use safety standards to ask precise questions

OSHA's construction focus materials address falls, struck-by incidents, caught-in or between hazards, and electrocution. Federal construction fall-protection rules set requirements for different conditions and tasks. Identify the work surface, height, task, and protective system in use before applying a provision. A safety standard can help identify relevant evidence. Who owed a duty and whether a condition caused an injury are separate questions under the applicable law. OSHA Construction Focus Four · OSHA fall protection standard, 29 C.F.R. § 1926.501

The Claim Evidence Organizer can match each issue to a record. The Accident Photo Checklist can help group existing photos by location and equipment. Follow site safety rules and preserve originals through an appropriate process.

Track benefits and payments without duplicating an expense

Keep medical payments, wage replacement, actual missed pay, and any third-party recovery separate. Where both a benefits claim and another claim exist, reimbursement or credit issues may affect the final distribution under state law. Confirm how each payment will be handled before accepting settlement terms.
