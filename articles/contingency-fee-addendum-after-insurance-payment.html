A Contingency Fee Addendum Came After One Insurance Payment

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

One insurer paid a claim under one coverage. Later you signed an addendum changing the contingency fee for your matter. Ask which recoveries each document covers and when the change took effect. The date of a payment and the description of the legal work covered by the agreement both matter to the accounting.

Collect the signed versions and the payment record

Save the original agreement, the full signed addendum and any correspondence explaining its effective date. Identify each payment by insurer, coverage, check or transfer date, amount and payee. Note whether it was deposited into a trust account or already disbursed. Ask for the existing settlement statement and any fee or cost entries tied to that payment.

California's State Bar describes contingency fees and the role of the fee agreement in determining what you pay. California Business and Professions Code § 6147 requires specified information for covered contingency agreements, including the rate and how costs and disbursements affect the fee and recovery. Check which jurisdiction and contract terms apply to your representation. State Bar of California fees and billing guide · California State Bar Act, § 6147

If your agreement applies a fee to only one recovery, calculate the fee on that specific coverage payment. If you paid a case cost early, track it separately from the amount withheld at closing.

Ask for the calculation for each recovery

For an illustration, the first carrier paid $15,000 before the addendum and a second paid $35,000 after it. Applying 25% to the first and 33% to the second produces fees of $3,750 and $11,550 before considering costs. Applying 33% to both produces a different total. Ask the lawyer to identify which signed provision supports each rate, what amount it applies to and how any costs enter the computation.

If the addendum says it covers further work or a different coverage, ask how the statement reflects that language. If it uses a date or trigger that could include the earlier payment, ask for the firm's written explanation and compare it with the signed copies. For an unresolved dispute, consult the applicable bar's fee dispute information or independent counsel.

Request an itemized statement

Use the Net Settlement Comparison Tool to compare the fee calculations and costs you were given. Keep different treatments as clearly labeled scenarios until the parties confirm the governing terms. If the release lists the wrong incident, see how to reconcile the draft with the offer. For a later installment payment, see which schedule establishes when it begins.
