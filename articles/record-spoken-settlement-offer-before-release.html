How to Record a Spoken Settlement Offer Before Seeing the Release

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An adjuster names a dollar amount by phone and says paperwork will follow. Record what you heard and ask for the exact written terms. The amount, the scope of the release and the process for payment each need their own confirmation.

Record the offer while the call is fresh

Write down the date, name of the person who made the offer, insurer, claim number, coverage and stated amount. Mark whether the figure was described as gross or as a payment after a deduction. Note any deadline, named payee or condition the adjuster mentioned, and keep track of which points still need confirmation.

A hypothetical note might say, “On July 15 the adjuster mentioned $4,600 to resolve the property-damage claim. They said paperwork would follow. I still need to confirm who would sign, which claim or coverage would be released, and how payment would be issued.” Use a dated record if you later need to show what was communicated. The NAIC recommends keeping insurer correspondence and call dates for complaints. NAIC guide to documenting claim communications

Ask for the full terms and supporting documents

You could reply, “Please send the complete written offer and proposed release, including all attachments. Please identify which claim and coverage the $4,600 applies to, the parties and claims named in the release, and any deadline or payment conditions.”

Read the proposed release together with any definitions and incorporated pages. California's Civil Code provides an example of a state contract interpretation rule that reads provisions together. If prior words or actions might have legal consequences, record them accurately and get advice on the applicable law. California rule on reading the contract as a whole

Record changes from the spoken to the written version

Keep the first note when the written terms arrive. Put the new amount, affected coverage and conditions in a second entry with the written date. If a term differs, ask which version the insurer intends to offer and request the correction in writing. Use the Insurance Settlement Offer Tracker to track each version. If the insurer mentioned an expiration without a date, ask for the exact time and terms of the offer. For multiple coverage payments, check which payment enters the fee calculation.
