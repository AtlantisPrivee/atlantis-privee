How to Challenge an Insurance Claim Denial

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 10, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Read the written denial and identify the claim number, date, coverage, and precise reason. Ask for any cited policy provision and supporting explanation you do not have. A document request, a partial payment, and a final denial can require different responses. Keep copies of the policy and each communication. NAIC guide to claim documentation

Match the evidence to the reason

  • A required document is missing. Compare the insurer's request with the document and proof of delivery.
  • The policy excludes the loss. Read the declarations, applicable policy version, and endorsements together.
  • The amount or cause is disputed. Check the itemized estimate, photographs, written valuation, or professional report behind the disputed figure.
  • A health plan denies a service. Match the EOB and denial to the plan language and any provider explanation.

Respond to the specific reason. If an EOB says a health claim was processed a certain way, verify the service date and billing entity before comparing it with a provider's statement. The Compare a Medical Bill With Your EOB tool organizes those amounts. An EOB and a bill serve different roles. CMS guide to reading an EOB

Follow the applicable review procedure

For auto or property insurance, use the claim or policy process described by the insurer and check the state insurance department's rules. Ask where to send your response, what review is available, and what date it is due. Keep a copy of the response and its receipt. NAIC guide to filing an insurance complaint

For health plans subject to applicable appeal protections, the denial notice may describe an internal appeal and external review. Some urgent situations have expedited processes. Verify your plan's applicable procedure and dates before filing. HealthCare.gov internal appeals · CMS action plan for a denied health insurance claim

Write a short response that can be checked

State the decision you are asking the insurer to reconsider, the reason it gave, the record that addresses that reason, and the action you are requesting. List attachments by date and title. If the issue is an inaccurate balance, include only the relevant comparison and ask for a corrected written decision. The Insurance Claim Follow-Up Email Builder provides a draft you can review and adjust.

If the insurer leaves a material question unanswered, use the Insurance Complaint Organizer to prepare a factual record for the regulator. See when a denial may raise a bad-faith question for the distinction between challenging the coverage decision and evaluating the conduct behind it. Keep legal and notice deadlines separate from your review requests.
