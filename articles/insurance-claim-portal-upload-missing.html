The Adjuster Says Your Portal Upload Is Missing

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A portal's confirmation is useful evidence of the submission you attempted. The practical problem is whether the correct documents reached the adjuster's view of the right claim. Solve that with a file-level inventory and a written acknowledgment. Re-uploading every attachment without a list makes it harder to tell whether the original packet was found.

Separate the upload from the attachment

Save the full confirmation screen or email, including its time, any submission ID and the claim number shown. Save the originals in the form you sent them. Build a short inventory with the exact file names, page counts and descriptions. If the receipt shows only one batch number, keep your inventory labeled as your own record of what you sent.

Consider an upload containing three PDFs. The receipt shows batch 8841 and the adjuster sees two PDFs. The difference could be one rejected file, a document assigned to a different claim activity, or a receipt that never identified its contents. Ask the adjuster to name the two readable attachments so you can isolate the third. If the company asks for a new copy, reference batch 8841 in the message and preserve both confirmations.

Ask for a readable copy check

Please confirm which documents from submission 8841 are visible and readable on claim 1234. My inventory shows estimate-v2.pdf (four pages), tow-receipt.pdf (one page) and photos-index.pdf (two pages). If a file is missing, please send the approved way to resend it and tell me which claim contact should receive it.

Use the Insurance Claim Follow-Up Email Builder to turn those details into a short message. Keep sensitive attachments inside the carrier's approved channel. If an active deadline is near, check it on the policy or notice and confirm the insurer's designated way to submit the material.

If the request comes from a new adjuster, share the original receipt and file list. For repeated requests, log each upload and confirm whether the attachment opens.

When the missing record matters to a decision

Ask for the written decision and identify the document the insurer says it lacked. In Washington, the current claim-file rule requires enough detail to reconstruct pertinent events and permits insureds to request portions of a first-party claim file at reasonable intervals, subject to specified limits. That provides a way to ask for the carrier's record of the submission. It gives no assurance that a particular portal log exists. Washington claim-file documentation rule

If the dispute stays unresolved, retain the receipt, inventory, messages and response as a single packet. The Washington insurance regulator explains its consumer complaint process and what it reviews. Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner's complaint process

For a denial that cites a different reason on review, compare both letters using the guide to changed denial reasons. For proof of which policy dates apply, see how to assemble a renewal record.
