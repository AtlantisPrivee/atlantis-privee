How to Verify a Dashcam Copy While Preserving the Original Card

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A dashcam clip can be useful evidence, and the original card may hold earlier or later segments and information about how those files were stored. Preserve both if possible. Decide whether you need a verified copy of a visible clip or a forensic acquisition of the whole card. The two give you different records.

Protect the card before making a working copy

Record the camera model, the card's label and capacity, when you received it and who handled it. Keep the card out of a dashcam that might record over older files. If the contents are disputed, the card is damaged or you need deleted files, ask a digital evidence examiner to acquire the card. A qualified examiner can document a read-only workflow, including write-blocking where appropriate. NIST's preservation guidance discusses storage media, preserving integrity and recording handling. NIST digital evidence preservation guide

Treat an export already obtained as its own record. Save the original export as received. Make a second copy for playback and note who supplied the export, how it was obtained and whether it came from the card, the dashcam application or a messaging service. When a camera provides several short segments, keep the adjacent files and their original names so the sequence can be checked.

Compare copies of the same file

Calculate a SHA-256 checksum for the preserved export and for the working copy. If the two hashes match, that is evidence that these files contain the same bytes. Record the file name, size, date of the check and both values. The SHA-256 File Checksum Tool can make that comparison without putting a private video on a public file-sharing site.

An exported MP4 and a card image serve different purposes. The MP4 contains one playable file. A forensic image can preserve the card's broader contents and storage structure, depending on how it was acquired. Two files that look the same during playback can have different hashes after conversion. Keep converted or annotated clips under distinct names and document their relationship to the source. SWGDE digital forensic video analysis practices

Preserve the sequence as well as the footage

Imagine the app shares only the minute containing impact. An earlier segment might show when the camera's clock changed, and a later one might contain a 911 call. Save any segment index and original export instructions alongside the clip you intend to share.
