A New Adjuster Asked for Records You Already Submitted

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

A new adjuster asks for the same photos or records you sent last week. Start with the receipt from your first submission. Give the new adjuster a way to find the documents and make it easy to identify anything the insurer still needs. A second submission can be useful when you label it as a copy and retain the earlier delivery history.

Give the adjuster enough information to locate the first upload

Find the prior email or portal confirmation. Record the claim number, recipient, date, confirmation reference and the attachment names. Confirm the correct insurer contact before sharing files, particularly if the packet includes medical information. Use the insurer's secure portal or another channel the carrier confirms.

Washington requires claim files to contain enough detail to reconstruct relevant events and dates. The NAIC's auto claim guide also explains how to document the loss and follow up with the assigned adjuster. Keep your own delivery record so you can identify a specific submission during a handoff. Washington claim-file documentation rule · NAIC guide to filing an auto claim

Send a request the adjuster can answer in one reply

A message might read, “On June 12 I uploaded the repair estimate dated June 10 and six passenger-side photos to claim 1234. The portal gave confirmation R57. Could you confirm which files are visible on your side and tell me which ones you need again?” If a file is genuinely missing, resend it and reference the first delivery in the message.

If the new adjuster actually needs a newer document, label it with the version or date. For example, send a revised estimate dated June 20 as a new version and state that the June 10 estimate was the earlier submission. Keep both if they explain why the amount changed.

Record the handoff and the reply

Use the Insurance Claim Follow-Up Email Builder to summarize the prior delivery and identify the action needed. Save any new confirmation alongside the old one. If the same file keeps being requested, keep a log of each resend and acknowledgment. If the new adjuster also lacks notes about prior decisions, rebuild the handoff from the dated record.
