A Diminished Value Estimate Compares Your Trade-In to Dealer Retail

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A dealer lists a clean-history car at $23,000 and offers $18,000 for your repaired car. The $5,000 difference contains more than the crash history. The first number is a retail asking price. The second is a proposed acquisition price and may be tied to a purchase transaction. To estimate a history-related difference, compare figures from the same side of the market.

Label each number by what it buys

Record whether each figure is a listing, completed sale, standalone purchase offer or trade allowance in a package deal. Include mileage, trim, region, condition and date. Get the offer in writing and ask whether it depends on financing or buying another vehicle. The FTC advises car shoppers to keep the purchase price and trade-in negotiations distinct enough to see when one figure offsets another. The same discipline helps identify what a trade-in quote can show. FTC guide to financing or leasing a car

Build two comparisons that can be checked

In one illustration, a buyer offers $18,000 for the repaired car and $19,400 for a genuinely comparable clean-history car under the same purchase terms. The $1,400 difference is a lead to investigate. Separately, similar cars list at $23,000 with clean history and $21,800 with disclosed comparable repairs. The $1,200 retail asking-price difference provides a second observation. Neither set establishes a final valuation without checking condition, date and the evidence behind the prices.

Ask the appraiser how they adjusted for prior damage, differences in mileage and repair quality, how they sourced the history descriptions and whether either comparison used actual sale data. Do not count outstanding repair defects as a history-only discount without identifying them. Put each comparable's source and limitations next to its price.

Check which kind of claim you have

In State Farm v. Mabry, the Georgia Supreme Court interpreted the policy at issue to include diminution in value within the loss assessment even after repair. That ruling is tied to the policy and law considered there. The Georgia insurance regulator's auto claim guidance is a starting point for the state process.
