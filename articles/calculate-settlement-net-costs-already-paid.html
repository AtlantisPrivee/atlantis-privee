How to Calculate Settlement Net When You Already Paid Some Costs

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Some case costs were paid from your own account before the settlement arrived. Identify those costs before comparing the closing statement with the check. The amount due at closing and your overall proceeds after expenses answer different questions.

Find who paid each invoice and whether a reimbursement is due

For each cost, write down the vendor, invoice number, total billed, amount you paid and amount the firm or another party paid. Keep the receipts and any credit or reimbursement on the same record. Ask the firm which remaining amounts will be charged to the proceeds and whether any sum will be paid back to you.

The State Bar of California explains that clients may pay costs directly or reimburse amounts advanced by their lawyer. It also recommends confirming whether costs affect the base used for a contingency fee. ABA Model Rule 1.5 describes how a covered fee agreement specifies expenses and the order of the fee calculation. Refer to the agreement and local rule for your case. California State Bar guide to case costs · ABA Model Rule 1.5 on contingency fees

Compute the closing check and the full cost separately

In a hypothetical example, the gross settlement is $25,000 and the fee is $7,500. You paid $180 for records before settlement and the firm advanced a $420 filing cost. Assume the agreed fee is calculated on gross and the prior $180 remains your expense. The check after the fee and the $420 reimbursement is $17,080. Your net after also accounting for the $180 you spent earlier is $16,900.

If the closing statement lists $600 as total case costs, ask for the breakdown of what was already paid and what will be withheld now. If the agreement or an adjustment reimburses your earlier payment, track that reimbursement as a separate credit and verify who receives it.

Make each receipt reconcile once

Use the Net Settlement Calculator with only the agreed inputs for the check you are modeling. Keep earlier out-of-pocket spending on its own line when comparing the broader result. For a fee charged against one payment, separate the percentage calculation from cost allocation. If the same invoice appears twice on the statement, compare the vendor records and payees.
