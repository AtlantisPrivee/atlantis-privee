The Appraisal Award Uses a Different Date From the Total Loss

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

An award dated August 18 can value a vehicle as of a May 2 loss. Those dates answer different questions. Before challenging an award, identify the effective valuation date in the appraiser's instructions or report. Then check the dates of the comparables used to reach that number.

Separate four dates on one page

Record the loss date, the date the appraiser was asked to value the vehicle, the date each comparable was available and the day the award was signed. A report may also contain a print date or the date a vendor retrieved its data. If the report says only “as of August 18,” ask the appraiser whether that refers to the effective date, the market snapshot or the document's preparation.

A May 2 collision, a June 5 appraisal demand, three listings dated May 8 through May 21 and an award signed August 18 are compatible with a May valuation. Replace those listings with August-only observations and the sourcing question changes. Ask the appraiser to show how those observations relate to the loss date under the governing policy and rules.

Compare the award with its underlying valuation

Washington's total-loss rule allows appraisal for a first-party dispute over actual cash value when the policy contains an appraisal provision. It directs the insurer to advise its appraiser of the state's total-loss calculations and sets requirements for the timing of comparable vehicles used in settlement offers. The rule permits certain comparables within a window around the loss, so a listing after the crash can still be relevant. Washington total-loss rule

Texas's insurance department separately describes appraisal as a way to resolve disagreement over how much a covered loss is worth. Read your own clause for the scope of the award and the procedure for a dispute. Texas insurance department guide to total-loss valuation disputes

Send a narrow request for the missing date

Please confirm the effective valuation date used for my vehicle, the dates the comparable vehicles were available, and whether the August 18 date reflects the award signature or the market date. If the effective date was different from May 2, please identify the instruction or policy language used.

Use the Records Request Letter Builder to specify the award, appraisers' valuation reports and comparable sheets. Keep the exact policy and award with the request. If the discrepancy could affect a final award, confirm any deadlines for disputing it under the applicable law before waiting on an informal answer.

If the records also omit installed features, see the adaptive equipment valuation guide. If the amount is settled and only the lender on the check is wrong, separate the payment problem from the appraisal.

For a vehicle that was already sold, document its actual availability and the price the vendor used. How to check a historical comparable in a total-loss report.
