Pedestrian Accident Claims: What to Do and What to Document

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 25, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

After a vehicle strikes a pedestrian, immediate safety comes first. Call 911 for injuries or immediate danger. Get to a safe place if you can do so safely and follow emergency responders' instructions. Ask someone else to record the vehicle or witness details if you need care. NHTSA pedestrian safety guidance · California Department of Insurance crash guide

Record the crossing as it was that day

An intersection name alone rarely tells the story. Record the side of the street, direction of travel, location of the impact, lane markings, nearby signals, and any sight obstruction. At dusk or night, note working streetlights and lighting conditions. If a witness saw the signal change, record what they observed and from where. A later photo of the intersection can explain the layout. Keep the date of the photo so a reader can distinguish it from the original scene.

Write down the police report number and the names of any businesses or transit operators with cameras in view. Video may have a short retention period. A written preservation request can identify a time window, location, and camera direction. Keep a copy of the request and any response. Use the Accident Photo Checklist and Insurance Claim Timeline Builder to organize material you already have.

Work out whose conduct and whose coverage are in question

The driver's turn, speed, lookout, and signal use can matter. The pedestrian's route and signal phase may also matter under the applicable rules. NHTSA describes safety responsibilities for both drivers and people walking. State law supplies the duties and rules used in an individual claim. Where a public agency's design or maintenance may be involved, check promptly for a separate government claim procedure. California's courts, for example, explain that many personal injury claims against a government agency require an initial claim within six months. California Courts guide to government claims

Keep a coverage list as well as a fault list. Ask whether the driver's liability policy, a work-vehicle policy, or coverage available to the injured person may apply. A person walking can have auto-policy questions about insured status and the circumstances of the collision. Ask the insurer to point to the relevant policy provisions in writing. If the driver left the scene, see the hit-and-run coverage guide. California Department of Insurance auto coverage guide

Document how the injury affected the days after the crash

Keep the first medical evaluation and the follow-up instructions. Save the date and source of work restrictions, any documented change in duties, and the costs you actually incurred. Compare a medical bill with the insurer's explanation of benefits for the same service before treating the amount on the bill as your remaining balance. CMS guide to comparing a bill and an EOB

The lost wages guide explains how to turn schedules and pay records into an estimate someone else can check. If recovery affects a daily task such as stairs or walking to a bus stop, the Daily Activity Recovery Journal can record the change over time.
