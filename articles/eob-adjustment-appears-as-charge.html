An EOB Adjustment Appears as a Charge on the Revised Bill

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

The insurance notice shows an adjustment. The revised provider statement lists the same amount as a positive charge. Ask the billing office whether that amount is an adjustment reversal, an informational entry or a new charge added to the patient balance. A label can mean different things in an insurer's explanation and the provider's ledger.

Trace the entries in posting order

Request the itemized bill and an account history for the affected service. Record the original charge, the allowed amount if shown, insurer payment, patient share, provider adjustment and any later reversal. Put each entry beside its posting date, description and sign. Ask the billing office how it represents credits and which entries affect the amount due.

CMS's Medicare remittance guidance describes group codes that assign responsibility for adjustments. For example, CO identifies a contractual obligation and PR identifies patient responsibility in that setting. Your EOB and the billing office's own posting explanation are the records to compare for your plan. CMS payment and remittance advice guide

Check the balance after the whole sequence

For an example, the original charge is $300, the plan allows $120, pays $90 and assigns $30 to the patient. A $180 contractual adjustment accounts for the difference between charge and allowed amount. If a later statement shows $180 as a debit, ask which original entry it reversed and whether a replacement adjustment posted. Compare the net patient amount at the end of the sequence with the active EOB. CMS guide to allowed charges and the patient balance

Ask for a dated ledger with each reversal and replacement matched to the relevant claim. If the provider still expects payment, ask which active plan decision or charge explains the amount and request an updated statement.

If the statement shows only the new debit, request the earlier transaction and its offset on the same page. Comparing both entries makes it easier to see whether the original adjustment has been reversed and how the replacement changes the running balance.

Record what remains due

Use the medical bill and EOB comparison tool for the current amounts and retain the intermediate entries as supporting records. For an entire claim that was reversed and reissued, pair the notices with the provider's postings. For a separate charge the plan treated as included in another service, review the bundled service decision.
