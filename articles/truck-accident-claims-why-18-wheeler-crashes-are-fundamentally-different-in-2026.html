Truck Accident Claims: Carrier Records, Coverage, and Evidence

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 15, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A truck can carry one company's name on its door, another company's trailer, and a load arranged by a third business. Start by identifying what each business did on the trip. Keep the tractor and trailer identifiers, carrier name, USDOT number, and any trip or shipment number in separate fields. FMCSA's public snapshot can help identify a registered carrier and its reported operations. Treat it as a lead and verify who ran the trip. FMCSA Company Snapshot

Identify the decision each record can explain

  • A dispatch message or rate confirmation may identify the assignment, timing, and parties who arranged the load.
  • A driver log and supporting records can help establish time on duty and time driving. Compare them with dispatch and location data for the same trip.
  • An inspection or maintenance record may show when someone observed a defect and what they did next. Match the unit number to the vehicle involved.

Federal hours-of-service rules differ by operation. For covered property-carrying drivers, FMCSA describes an ordinary 11-hour driving limit after 10 consecutive hours off duty and a 14-hour window, with exceptions. Check which rule applied on the trip before labeling a log entry a violation. A regulatory issue and the cause of a crash are distinct questions. FMCSA hours-of-service summary

Preserve original files and their time references

Record the carrier, investigator, or agency holding each file and request appropriate preservation promptly. Save the original format and any export notes. A truck module's recorded event may have a different trigger from the first visible impact. The Swanton toll plaza investigation illustrates why time zero can mark a different event across sources.

Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to connect each observation to its original source. A checksum can help identify whether two copies have the same bytes. The SHA-256 File Checksum Tool computes that value locally. A matching checksum says nothing about when the original recording was made.

Read the applicable insurance filing and policy separately

FMCSA's filing table uses operation type, vehicle weight, and cargo. For example, it lists a $750,000 minimum for a for-hire property carrier operating vehicles of at least 10,001 pounds GVWR that transports nonhazardous property. Other operations can have different requirements. Use the applicable policy and filings to establish the actual coverage in a case. FMCSA insurance filing requirements

If a freight broker arranged the load, separate the driver's conduct from the broker's selection decision. The Montgomery freight broker case explains how a federal preemption question can be resolved before a court reaches the underlying negligence evidence. Bring a trip-specific timeline, policy documents, and a list of missing records to any review of the claim.
