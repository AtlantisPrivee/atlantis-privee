Documenting Damaged Aftermarket Panniers in a Motorcycle Claim

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A motorcycle repair estimate may include a cracked rack and leave the cases off the sheet. Panniers, racks and the belongings inside them are separate things to document. Ask which policy section the insurer is applying to each, then show the reviewer exactly what was mounted on the bike and damaged in the crash.

Identify the case and hardware individually

List the left and right cases, lids or locks, mounting plate, rack and any hardware the shop says must be replaced. Note the part number, purchase date, finish and mounting system. Photograph damaged surfaces and the points where the cases attach to the motorcycle. If the cases came with a used bike and you have no separate invoice, ask for the seller's listing, bill of sale, dealership accessory records or another dated image that shows the same items installed.

Have the shop say whether a case, its lid or a mounting component can be replaced separately. If the repair estimate uses a kit price, ask which parts the kit includes so the same bracket is not included a second time. Save a written parts quote identifying the specific models. Enter the items and supporting documents in the Claim Expense and Receipt Tracker.

Ask how the policy values each item

Washington's insurance regulator distinguishes liability for damage to others from physical damage coverage such as collision. As a carrier-specific example, Progressive's motorcycle accessory guide lists saddlebags among items that accessory coverage can include. Read the declaration and endorsement for your own bike to identify eligible equipment, the limit, any deductible and the value basis. Ask which provisions apply to gear or contents that were inside the cases. Washington insurance regulator's auto coverage guide · Progressive's motorcycle accessory coverage description

Consider a hypothetical damaged right case costing $310 to replace, a $65 bracket and an intact left case. The parts request starts with the two damaged components. If only a complete pair is available, attach the supplier's response and ask how the carrier handles the set. A pair's price is useful when it explains the parts constraint and why a standalone replacement cannot be sourced.

Send one list with an answerable question

Ask the adjuster to identify which items appear in the bike estimate, which are considered under accessory coverage and which need more documentation. If the motorcycle is a total loss, ask whether covered accessories are already in the vehicle valuation. Keep a dated copy of the inventory and any written decisions.

If the shop added a separate operation after the first estimate, follow how to document a missing supplement invoice. If weather or storage changed the appearance of the gear, see how to compare the first and later condition photos. For the coverage background, see the motorcycle claims guide.
