A Delivery Worker Was Bitten at a New York Gate. What Can the Latch Show?

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A package is marked delivered and a dog reaches the worker on the way out. One person remembers the gate closing. Another says the latch had been sticking. To make the condition of the gate useful in a claim, connect its operation and repair history to the route people were expected to take.

A New York delivery case puts restraint in focus

In Flanders v. Goodfellow, a postal carrier was delivering a package when the owners' dog escaped past a person through an open front door and bit her. The case concerned a doorway. Its reasoning makes it useful to a different entrance dispute involving a gate. New York's Court of Appeals recognized that an owner can face a common-law negligence claim and reinstated both negligence and strict-liability claims on the record before it. The court sent the case back for further proceedings. New York Court of Appeals opinion in Flanders

Strict liability and negligence present distinct proof questions. The opinion discusses the owner's actual or constructive knowledge of dangerous propensities for the strict-liability theory. An inquiry about a defective latch instead asks who controlled the restraint, what they knew about its operation and how the dog reached the worker. New York's dangerous-dog statute also addresses medical costs in specified circumstances. New York Agriculture and Markets Law § 123

Photograph the route and preserve the condition

From a place you are authorized to be, document the delivery path, gate swing, latch, keeper plate, warning signs and visible wear. If a camera captured the delivery, ask its custodian for a copy around the time shown in the delivery scan. A single photo of a gate resting closed may leave open whether it latched and how it opened. Keep any later repair or adjustment date with the corresponding photos.

Ask who owns or maintains the gate and who usually uses it. A dated complaint that the latch failed to catch can answer a different question from a report about prior lunging or escape. Save the delivery instructions, scanner time, route note and available witness statements. For an employee injured during a delivery, preserve the incident and employer reports alongside the property records.

Label what each record can establish

A latch invoice can identify the part and repair date. A witness can place the dog and worker on either side of the gate. A time-stamped video may show when the gate swung and whether anyone handled it. Enter those separate questions in the Claim Evidence Organizer and add the record that answers each.

If you need footage from a doorbell or exterior camera, use the native video request checklist. If a witness saw the approach from across the street, adapt the vantage-point questions for a crash witness. See also the site's overview of dog bite claims.
