When Your Dashcam Clock Disagrees With the 911 Call Time

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Your dashcam stamps the impact at 6:14:10 p.m. A dispatch record puts the first call later. Both times may be useful. First identify the event each timestamp describes. A crash, the start of a phone call, the call being answered and a unit being dispatched can all happen at different moments.

Ask what each time means

Save the dashcam file in its original format and note whether the time appears on the image, in the file information or in the viewer. Write down the displayed time zone and any device setting you can verify. Keep the original display in your notes even after you estimate a correction.

Ask the agency that holds the 911 records which times it records and how it labels them. Depending on the system, the useful records may include call audio, computer-aided dispatch entries and a description of the fields. Include the address, local date, time window and incident number when making the request. Anne Arundel County, Maryland, publishes an example of an agency-specific request process. Anne Arundel County 911 records instructions

Compare the clocks using the same event

Suppose the dashcam audio records a speakerphone connection at 6:14:32 p.m. and the agency confirms that its 6:15:14 p.m. entry marks that same connection. The agency clock in that example is 42 seconds ahead of the dashcam. Applying that offset to the dashcam's 6:14:10 impact gives an estimated agency-clock time of 6:14:52. State which event you matched and label the result an estimate.

If the agency's entry actually marks when the caller dialed or when a dispatcher opened the incident, that subtraction would mix different events. Seek another shared cue or leave the offset unresolved. When possible, test the offset against a second event. SWGDE's video acquisition guidance recommends documenting a recorder's time against a reference clock and recording the difference. SWGDE digital recorder acquisition guidance

Share the raw and adjusted times together

Keep a row for each event with the source, original timestamp, time zone, meaning of the field and estimated corrected time. Attach the excerpt that explains each field. Use the Insurance Claim Timeline Builder to keep the record of the impact distinct from the record of the call.
