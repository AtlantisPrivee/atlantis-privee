The Provider Moved Your Balance to a New Account After EOB Reprocessing

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

The provider reprocesses a bill and opens a new account number. The old and new screens both show a balance. Ask for the transaction that connects them. The useful records are the transfer out, the transfer in and the current ledger entries that explain whether any amount appears twice.

Ask what moved and why

Gather the older statement, the revised explanation of benefits, or EOB, and the latest statement. Record the old and new account numbers, service date, billing entity, active claim reference and each statement date. Ask whether the provider moved the full account or specific lines, and request the transfer date, amount and reason for each. CMS's medical bill guide explains why the account identifier and current balance are useful when asking about a statement. CMS guide to reading a medical bill

Ask how the provider handled payments you made before the move. Request the amount posted on the original account, the amount carried to the new account and any other transfers or refunds. Keep both ledgers until you can match the entries.

Reconcile the same obligation across two accounts

For an example, the first account held $180 after insurance. The provider transfers $120 to the new account after a revised decision and removes the remaining $60. Verify the $120 transfer out, its entry on the new account and the $60 adjustment. If an earlier statement still displays $180, ask the provider to issue dated balances for both accounts.

If a covered provider holds billing and payment records about you, HHS explains the right to access those records, subject to applicable exceptions. If a covered debt collector contacts you, CFPB explains the validation information it generally must provide, including itemization of payments and credits. Match the collector's reference to the provider's accounting. HHS right of access to billing and payment records · CFPB debt validation guide

Keep one record of the amount still due

Use the medical bill and EOB comparison tool with the active EOB and both ledgers. Ask the billing office to confirm in writing which account receives future payments. If you had an installment agreement, reconcile the plan's balance with the transferred charges. If the insurer reversed a claim before the move, track the reversal and replacement postings.
