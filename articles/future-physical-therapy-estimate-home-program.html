Your Future Physical Therapy Estimate Changed to a Home Program

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An early estimate assumes weekly physical therapy for three months. Your clinician later recommends a home program with a check-in. Update the future-cost calculation using the current plan. Keep care already received, recommended future care and possible care if symptoms change on separate lines.

Find the written change and the remaining services

Request the earlier and current plan or discharge note. Record when the change was made, what supervised visits remain, what follow-up is recommended, and which equipment or instruction is part of the new plan. Ask whether the check-in is scheduled, contingent on progress or already included in another visit. Collect written prices from the appropriate provider or supplier.

The Medicare Benefit Policy Manual discusses recording therapy type, amount, frequency and duration and documenting changes to the plan. That is Medicare guidance. The dated clinical record provides the basis for describing the care recommended in your own case. CMS Medicare Benefit Policy Manual, chapter 15, § 220.1.2

Reprice the remaining plan line by line

Suppose the earlier projection listed 12 visits at $110 each, for $1,320. The updated plan specifies two $110 follow-ups and a $45 piece of equipment. The updated future total for those items is $265. Keep the cost of any visits already attended in the past-care record. If the therapist recommends more appointments only if function worsens, list the trigger and the source as a separate scenario.

State whether a figure is a provider estimate, an insurance copayment or another expected out-of-pocket amount. Use comparable price types when totaling a scenario. California's civil jury instruction on future medical expenses illustrates the distinction between reasonably necessary future care and its reasonable cost. The applicable damages rules depend on the jurisdiction and record. California civil jury instruction on medical expenses, CACI No. 3903A

Tie every cost to the current plan

Use the Future Care Cost Planner for the dated current recommendation and its unit prices. Attach the old estimate for comparison and note which items changed. For details on when the change was entered in the chart, read the medical record addendum guide. If you need to document how long you can perform the exercises or another daily activity, see how to record duration, breaks and assistance.
