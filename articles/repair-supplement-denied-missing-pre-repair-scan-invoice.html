A Repair Supplement Was Denied for a Missing Pre-Repair Scan Invoice

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

The shop says it scanned the car. The insurer says the supplement arrived without the invoice. Before debating the price, find out whether the missing record is the scan report, the invoice for a subcontracted scan or an in-house labor line on the shop's final bill. Those documents answer different questions.

Match proof of the work to proof of the charge

Ask for the estimate version and disputed line number. Then request the scan printout or electronic report with the VIN, scan time and results. Ask for the corresponding itemized charge and repair order. For an outsourced scan, identify the vendor invoice the shop received. For an in-house scan, ask the shop to point to its own documentation and billing line. A scan report can show that diagnostics ran. It may not identify the amount charged for that operation.

California's repair regulations describe authorization for extra work and what belongs on an invoice for shops governed by those rules. Use the records to show which charge the shop documented. Payment still depends on the coverage and the insurer's review of the charge. California's additional repair authorization rule · California's repair invoice rule

Link the operation to the vehicle's procedure

American Honda's collision position statement specifies a preliminary diagnostic scan during estimating and a scan after repair for vehicles within its scope. For a specific repair, match the manufacturer's guidance to the vehicle and the damage involved. Attach the relevant procedure or page with the corresponding scan report. American Honda's scan requirements, reproduced by CCC

Consider an example where a supplement lists a $125 pre-scan and a $160 post-scan. The insurer acknowledges the post-scan invoice and leaves the pre-scan blank. Submit the earlier time-stamped report and the pre-scan charge together. Label the two operations separately so the reviewer can match each charge to its own report. The Auto Repair Estimate Comparison tool can help align the lines across versions.

Ask the insurer to classify the decision

Request a short written answer identifying whether the line is pending documentation or declined after the carrier reviewed the documentation. If the denial is about rate, necessity, coverage or duplicate billing, ask for that reason and the record the reviewer used. Save the dated supplement and the response together.

If the carrier cannot locate the attachments, use the portal receipt workflow. If it issues a new reason after receiving them, preserve both decisions using the denial comparison guide.
