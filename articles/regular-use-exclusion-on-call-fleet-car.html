A Fleet Car Was Assigned Only During On-Call Weeks. Does Regular Use Apply?

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

An insurer invokes a regular-use exclusion after a crash in a work car. You drove the fleet vehicle during one on-call week each month. Document how the assignment actually worked. Frequency matters to the factual record, and access between trips can be just as important when the policy speaks of a vehicle furnished or available for regular use.

Describe access as precisely as mileage

Start with the exclusion, definitions and coverage endorsement in force on the crash date. Note whether it refers to a specific vehicle, a vehicle regularly used, or vehicles furnished or available for regular use. Check the VIN and which coverage the insurer is reviewing.

Ask the employer for the on-call calendar, fleet checkout logs, key records, rules for taking the car home and permission to use it outside work. A rotating assignment could involve one permanently assigned car with restricted use, or a different car that the driver can only obtain for a specific shift. Document who could approve an exception and whether the worker actually kept the keys off rotation.

Compare the pattern with the decision the insurer cites

In Rush v. Erie Insurance Exchange, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court upheld the regular-use exclusion against a challenge under Pennsylvania's motor vehicle insurance law. The case involved a detective using an employer-owned police vehicle. Read the opinion alongside the language of your endorsement and the facts about your own access. Pennsylvania's statute separately describes the state's rules for offering uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. Pennsylvania Supreme Court opinion in Rush · 75 Pa.C.S. § 1731

For an example, a technician takes a car home Monday through Sunday during a recurring rotation and returns the keys at the end of that week. Put the calendar and key receipt together. If the insurer describes the car as continuously available, ask it to identify the records supporting access between rotations. If it relies on repeated access during each assigned week, ask for the part of the endorsement that applies to that pattern.

Send the comparison with the claim number

A short request can identify the exclusion, state the documented limits on access and ask whether the insurer disputes those facts or interprets the policy differently. Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to attach the policy, schedule and key record to the corresponding points.

For the complete background to the Pennsylvania case, read how the work-car policy dispute arose. If coverage applies and the dispute instead concerns liability payments, see how to check a UIM credit after two drivers pay.
