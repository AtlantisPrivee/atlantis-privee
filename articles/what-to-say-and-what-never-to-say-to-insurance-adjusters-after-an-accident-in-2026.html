Talking to an Insurance Adjuster After a Crash

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Before an adjuster call, confirm the company, claim number, and coverage under review. Your own insurer may ask for information under the policy's notice and cooperation provisions. A request from another party's insurer may raise different questions. Ask what is required, under which policy or procedure, and by when. Keep any deadline in writing. California Department of Insurance auto claims guide

Use direct answers and keep uncertainty visible

Provide the basic facts you know, such as the date, location, vehicles, and report number. When you do not remember a detail, say so. For example, “I saw the vehicle enter the intersection. I don't know its speed.” A photograph or video may later supply more information. Keep the first account and any correction with their own dates. NAIC auto claim documentation guidance

If asked how you are doing, describe current observations accurately and distinguish them from medical conclusions. “My evaluation is scheduled for Tuesday” is more precise than guessing about diagnosis or recovery. Share relevant medical or employment records through the agreed claim process and keep a copy of what you sent.

Clarify a request for a recorded statement or authorization

Ask who will record it, what issues it covers, whether a copy or transcript is available, and when a response is due. Ask for the policy basis if your insurer treats it as required. A broad authorization to obtain records may have a different scope from supplying a specific report or bill. Ask which providers, dates, and records the request covers. If there is a dispute about the scope or obligation, a local lawyer can explain the applicable terms and law. California Department of Insurance guide to reporting a claim

Use the Compare Two Claim Statements tool to compare a transcript or summary with your notes. A difference may be a simple mistake or a point that needs clarification. Save both versions with the date and origin of each.

Close the call with a checkable next step

Write down the requested document, who will send it, and any confirmed date. For example, ask “Can you confirm whether you need the itemized bill, the EOB, or both?” The Insurance Claim Follow-Up Email Builder can turn that into a short message you can review before sending. NAIC guidance on keeping claim correspondence

If the adjuster mentions a “final offer,” ask for the amount, deadline, and proposed release in writing. See five insurance requests worth clarifying for document-by-document questions and the denied claim guide if you receive a written adverse decision.

If the insurer says an upload is missing, match your portal receipt to the adjuster's list of readable files. If it issues a different denial after reviewing the file, compare the two reasons in writing.
