A Provider Refund Appears in Your Settlement Before It Arrives

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Your settlement breakdown includes a provider refund you have yet to receive. Ask whether the entry is an expected credit, an actual deposit or a number used to estimate a future balance. Identify who is supposed to receive the money and where it will be recorded when it arrives.

Find the transaction the provider is reversing

Gather the original receipt, the revised explanation of benefits if there is one, and any refund confirmation. Ask the provider to give the amount, payee, method, issue date and transaction reference. Verify whether a card refund goes to the original card, whether a check is being mailed, or whether the provider made a credit within its own account.

CMS explains how an EOB's patient balance can include amounts already paid, and how a provider statement records the payments and adjustments on that account. Match the entries to the account that generated the original payment. CMS explanation of benefits guide · CMS guide to medical bill transactions

Reconcile the current amount and the expected credit

Suppose you previously paid $600 and the provider agrees to return $250 to your original card. The refund has been approved but has yet to post. If the provider is paying you directly, record the $250 separately from the funds the firm has received. Ask the firm what its statement means if the refund appears on a line that affects the check. If the provider plans to pay the firm, ask for the client ledger entry after the deposit and an explanation of who receives the resulting credit.

California's professional conduct rules include requirements for recordkeeping and accounting for funds lawyers hold. Use the current client accounting to compare a pending provider promise with an actual receipt in trust. California Rule 1.15 on handling funds

Update the reconciliation when the refund posts

Use the Insurance Claim Cash Flow Planner to record the expected date, then enter the posting date and actual payee. If the provider reduced the bill without issuing cash, compare the write-off and actual payoff on separate rows. If an amount moved through the firm, match the deposit and disbursement to the trust ledger.
