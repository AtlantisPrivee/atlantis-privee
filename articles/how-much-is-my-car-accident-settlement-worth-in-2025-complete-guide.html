Car Accident Settlement Amounts: How to Read an Offer

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 9, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A settlement offer is easier to evaluate once you can explain each line. Start with the written proposal and identify which person, insurer, coverage, and losses it addresses. A payment to repair a vehicle and a release of bodily injury claims can resolve different issues. Keep the relevant policy, offer, and proposed release together. California Department of Insurance auto claim guide

Build the loss record from documents you can check

For medical expenses, group the itemized provider bill, explanation of benefits, and actual payments by service date and provider. Record the billed amount, plan adjustments, and what remains separately. An EOB describes how the plan processed the charge. The provider can explain a remaining balance or payment that has not posted. CMS medical bill and EOB guidance

For past missed work, use a schedule, pay rate, employer confirmation, and the dates of any documented restriction. Track paid leave separately. For a future cost, note what care or service it represents, who recommended it, how often it is expected, and the source for the price. The lost wages guide and long-term injury cost guide show how to build both records.

Keep repair or valuation documents in a property file. A total-loss report should be read alongside the condition and options recorded for the car. If a comparable vehicle differs, ask what adjustment the insurer made and why. The Washington total-loss case explains the difference between a vehicle-condition deduction and the other amounts that may affect a check.

If the collision involved a motorcycle, keep any damaged riding gear and its receipts on separate lines. The motorcycle claim guide explains how to document the bike and equipment and check the coverage for each.

Identify what the offer leaves unresolved

Coverage limits, disputes about fault or causation, and state rules about recoverable damages affect how an offer is evaluated. Read any proposed release for the parties, claims, and time period it covers. For harm such as pain or loss of enjoyment, use records of the activities affected and the duration rather than turning an expense total into a universal multiplier. California's civil jury instructions, for example, direct jurors to evaluate noneconomic loss from the evidence without a fixed calculation. California Civil Jury Instructions, CACI 3905A

Calculate the net under a written set of assumptions

Suppose an illustrative $40,000 injury offer has an entered $10,000 fee, $1,200 in costs, and a $3,800 confirmed repayment. That leaves $25,000 under those assumptions. If the fee agreement uses a different calculation base, or a repayment remains disputed, change those inputs and keep both scenarios. The example is arithmetic and gives no estimate of a particular claim.

The Net Settlement Calculator and Net Settlement Offer Comparison make the deductions visible. Review the Medicare recovery case if a payment list and a final demand show different amounts. Compare both the proposed net and the terms you would be accepting. California State Bar guide to fees and costs
