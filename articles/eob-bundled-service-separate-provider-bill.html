The EOB Says a Service Was Bundled but the Provider Billed It Separately

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

A health plan lists a service as included in another procedure. The provider statement still requests a separate payment for the line. Compare the exact claim and the provider's coding and posting record. The amount a plan declines to pay separately and the amount it assigns to the patient are different fields.

Locate the bundled line and the service it was combined with

Write down the provider, service date, claim reference, line description and adjustment reason. Ask the plan which other line it treated as including the service and how it assigned responsibility for the denied or adjusted amount. Ask whether the current decision applies to the entity that sent the bill. A facility and a professional group can each bill related work.

CMS's National Correct Coding Initiative describes Medicare edits that can prevent separate payment for certain code pairs under defined conditions. The edit and plan rules that apply to your claim should come from the payer. Medicare's remittance guidance also explains how group codes describe who is assigned a financial obligation in that program. CMS procedure-to-procedure edits · CMS Medicare remittance guidance

Ask why the charge is still on the bill

For an illustration, the EOB shows a $40 patient amount for a procedure and a second line with zero patient responsibility because it was included in the first. The provider asks for $40 plus $75 for the second service. Ask it to reconcile the $75 with the decision, confirm whether it posted the adjustment, and identify the billing basis for any amount it still expects.

Request a new itemized statement if the provider updates the claim. If the payer says the line was processed under a different provider or benefit, ask it to confirm the reference and any new responsibility. CMS's consumer EOB guide explains which fields to match with the bill. CMS explanation of benefits guide

Save the answer with the specific service line

Use the medical bill and EOB comparison tool to compare the active plan amount with what the billing office requests. If an adjustment appears as a positive charge, track its debit and credit entries. If a copay remains after the decision assigns zero, reconcile the charge with the current notice.
