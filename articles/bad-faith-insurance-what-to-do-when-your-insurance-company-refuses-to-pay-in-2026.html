When an Insurance Denial Raises a Bad Faith Question

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Start with the exact reason the insurer gave. A policy exclusion, an expired policy, a missing record, and a dispute about who caused a loss are different reasons for a denial. Ask for the provision and facts the insurer relied on. Keep the policy version, declarations, endorsements, and denial together. California Department of Insurance fair claims settlement regulations

Read one reason against one policy provision

Make a table with the insurer's reason, relevant clause, document the insurer relied on, and your question. If the letter says a vehicle was used for work, ask which endorsement or exclusion applies to that vehicle, person, and trip. The Pennsylvania work-car coverage case illustrates why the precise policy wording and vehicle use can determine one coverage layer.

If the insurer says a document was missing, provide the record and transmission evidence if you have them. If you disagree with a condition adjustment or repair figure, request the report and identify the line you dispute. Use the Insurance Claim Follow-Up Email Builder to draft a concise request with the claim reference and document dates. NAIC auto claim guidance

Document how the insurer reached the decision

Record the first report, documents requested, submission dates, changes in explanation, and the final decision. A mistaken decision can be different from conduct that meets a state's bad-faith standard. Florida, for example, states that negligence alone is insufficient under its bad-faith statute and describes specific notice and tender provisions. A lawyer can evaluate whether the rule applies to the relationship and timing in your case. Florida Statutes § 624.155

The claim-handling timeline guide shows how to preserve what each party knew and when. Focus the timeline on the decision in dispute. A letter dated March 2 may have been received on March 6. Keep both dates when the distinction matters.

Keep review, complaints, and legal deadlines separate

Read the denial letter and applicable rules for reconsideration or appeal. A state insurance department can receive a complaint about insurer conduct. A complaint may serve a different purpose from challenging the benefit decision or filing a lawsuit. Save the complaint confirmation and track other deadlines by their own source. NAIC consumer complaint guidance

For health-plan claims, the applicable internal appeal and independent external review procedures can differ from auto or property disputes. Review the step-by-step denial guide for how to identify the correct process. HealthCare.gov guide to internal appeals
