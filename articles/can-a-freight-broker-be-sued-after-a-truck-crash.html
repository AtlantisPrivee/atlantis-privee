Can a Freight Broker Be Sued After a Truck Crash?

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

The name on a tractor door identifies only one link in a shipment. When a truck hauling plastic pots struck a tractor-trailer stopped beside an Illinois road, the injured driver asked a question that went further upstream. Who picked the company hauling the load, and what did it know when it picked that company? U.S. Supreme Court opinion in Montgomery

That question reached the U.S. Supreme Court in Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC. On May 14, 2026, the Court unanimously held that a state-law negligent hiring claim against a freight broker for selecting a motor carrier falls within the federal motor vehicle safety exception at issue. The ruling sends the dispute back for further proceedings. Whether the broker actually made a negligent selection remains a separate issue. Supreme Court opinion, slip opinion pp. 1–4, 9–10

The decision that happened before the collision

Caribe Transport II was the motor carrier. Its driver was hauling the shipment when his truck struck Shawn Montgomery's stopped rig. C.H. Robinson had arranged the shipment as a broker. Montgomery sued the driver, carrier, broker, and related entities. His claim against the broker focused on its selection of the carrier. U.S. Supreme Court opinion in Montgomery

Montgomery alleged that the carrier had a conditional federal safety rating when selected. The Supreme Court described the allegation as part of the claim it was reviewing. To decide whether the selection was negligent, a court would still need evidence about the rating, the time at which it applied, and the broker's conduct under the applicable state law. Supreme Court opinion, slip opinion pp. 3–4

That distinction matters when reading the docket. A court can describe an allegation in enough detail to decide whether the law permits a claim to proceed without treating the allegation as a proven finding. The strongest factual sentence to write from this record is that Montgomery alleged a conditional rating at the time of selection.

Why federal trucking law was the first hurdle

The Federal Aviation Administration Authorization Act limits state regulation relating to a broker's prices, routes, or services. Section 14501(c)(2)(A) preserves state safety authority with respect to motor vehicles. The lower courts held that the federal law barred Montgomery's claim. The Supreme Court held that the safety exception covered the negligent hiring claim before it. It assumed for that analysis that the general preemption provision would otherwise apply. 49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)

Think of the appeal as a question about the door to the courthouse. Behind that door are different questions. What duty did the broker owe under state law? What would a reasonable selection process have shown? Could an alleged lapse in selecting the carrier be connected to this crash and these injuries? The Supreme Court resolved the federal statutory issue. Those other questions turn on the case record and the applicable law.

Follow the load through three records

  • Dispatch records connect a driver and vehicle to a particular trip. A tractor registration or a photo of a logo can help identify the equipment. A dispatch record can put that tractor and driver on the actual shipment.
  • Shipment records identify the parties and their roles. A bill of lading, load confirmation, and broker-carrier agreement can help establish who moved the goods and who arranged the movement. Compare legal names and identifiers across the documents, especially where a brand name appears on more than one company's paperwork.
  • Selection records address the decision challenged in the lawsuit. An onboarding file, dated safety check, or approval log may show what the selector knew or checked before assigning the load. A record created after the crash should be labeled with its actual date.

The carrier's USDOT number is a useful link between records. The public FMCSA Company Snapshot can confirm the company you are looking at and show the latest safety information available there. The agency explains that the public snapshot does not maintain a historical safety-rating archive. Save the date of any lookup and keep it separate from evidence about the date the load was booked. FMCSA SAFER FAQ · FMCSA Company Snapshot

A small difference in dates changes the question

Consider an illustration unrelated to the parties in Montgomery. A shipment confirmation is dated March 3. A public carrier lookup from April 20 shows a conditional rating. The difference is 48 days. The April printout can identify a question about the March decision. Establishing the March rating requires a record for that earlier date.

The useful next entries in the file are the exact carrier name and USDOT number, the original assignment timestamp, the date any rating took effect, and the document that establishes each fact. Separate the question of what the regulator's records showed from the question of what the broker actually reviewed. A dated screening report can help answer the latter.

Start a record of the two timelines

One timeline covers the shipment, including broker assignment, carrier acceptance, and dispatch. The second covers the collision, including the vehicle locations, time, and witnesses. Give each entry a document reference. Keeping the timelines separate makes it easier to see whether an alleged earlier choice has a supported connection to what happened on the road.

Use the Evidence-to-Fact Map to label the document behind each assertion. Start with the identity of the carrier and the date it accepted the load.

Ask what the broker could see at the selection date

Request a record that identifies the carrier by USDOT number and shows when the broker chose it for that shipment. Then compare that timestamp with the effective date of any safety rating and with the version of the carrier information someone actually consulted. FMCSA's public records can help locate a carrier. A current snapshot and a contemporaneous selection record answer different questions. FMCSA company safety records
