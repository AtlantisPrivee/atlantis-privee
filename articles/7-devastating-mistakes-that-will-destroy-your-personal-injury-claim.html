Seven Recordkeeping Mistakes That Can Complicate an Injury Claim

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 11, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

An injury file is easier to review when another person can tell what happened, when it happened, and which source supports each statement. These seven common gaps are practical places to start.

Losing the report reference

Write down the agency, report number, and date. Keep a state reporting form, police report, and insurer claim reference separate. Different reports can serve different purposes. See the post-crash checklist for what to capture safely. California Department of Insurance crash reporting guide

Saving only edited copies

Keep original photos, videos, and messages with their dates. Use separate copies for crops or redactions. Identify who took a photo and whether it shows the incident or a later visit. The Accident Photo Checklist helps group the views. NAIC auto claim documentation guide

Leaving medical and work dates unexplained

Seek medical care when needed and follow the clinical guidance you receive. Keep dates of symptoms, visits, restrictions, and missed work. Some concussion symptoms can appear later. Discuss relevant changes with a healthcare provider. The lost wages guide shows how to match restrictions and actual pay records. CDC mild TBI symptoms guidance

Treating private posts as outside the record

Preserve relevant posts and messages in context. Changing visibility or deleting material can create separate issues when evidence needs to be preserved. In New York's Forman v. Henkin, the court considered targeted disclosure based on relevance to the injuries and limitations claimed. See the social media evidence case for what was ordered and why. New York Court of Appeals opinion

Giving a guess the status of a fact

If you cannot remember speed, distance, or timing, say so. Date any correction and explain its source. Ask what a recorded statement is for and whether your policy requires it. The adjuster conversation guide offers neutral ways to make the next step clear. NAIC claim communication guidance

Comparing gross offers alone

Ask for the release and written breakdown. Identify which claims the payment resolves and what costs or repayments may come out of it. Compare scenarios using the Net Settlement Offer Comparison tool. California State Bar guidance on fees and costs · CMS beneficiary recovery process

Combining all dates into one reminder

A time to respond to an offer, a policy notice, and a lawsuit filing date are different entries. Put the source beside each confirmed date, using the Insurance Claim Date and Reminder Planner if helpful. Check the filing deadline guide for examples of separate legal and government notice clocks. California Courts filing deadline guide
