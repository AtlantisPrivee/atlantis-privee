A Crash Witness Saw Red From the Opposite Side. Which Signal Was It?

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

“I saw red” is useful once the observation has a location and an object. At an intersection with multiple approaches and turn indications, a person standing across the road can see a signal that governs a different lane. Before turning the statement into a conclusion about a driver, identify the face the person remembers.

Draw the view before discussing fault

Ask the witness to mark their position on a neutral sketch, the direction they faced and where they first noticed the vehicle. Let them point to the remembered signal on a photo or drawing, and record whether they could see the front of that signal or only a reflection or side view. Ask whether they saw a circular light, an arrow or a pedestrian indication. Preserve uncertainty in their words.

The federal MUTCD differentiates vehicle indications such as circular red and red arrows, and separately describes pedestrian indications. For a particular intersection, check which movement the indicated face governed using the signal plan or a clear contemporaneous image. FHWA's current MUTCD, Part 4

Tie the red indication to a moment

A witness may remember red when they looked up after hearing the crash. Ask for the sequence in their own words. Did they see the light before the car entered the intersection, as it crossed the stop line, at impact or after they heard a sound? Record any interruption in their view and any timing source they actually used.

The U.S. Department of Justice's eyewitness guidance recommends starting with open-ended prompts and documenting what the witness recalls. Adapt that interview principle to a traffic observation. An invitation such as “Show me which light you saw and tell me what happened before you looked at it” leaves room for a more useful answer. NIJ guide to eyewitness evidence

Check whether a second record can identify the face

A photo of the northbound lanes may show two heads aligned from the witness's location. A dated phase map and lane diagram can help establish which one applied to the northbound turn lane. If a recording exists, compare the visible scene and the camera time with the witness's sequence. Keep observations about placement, color and timing distinct in the file.

Use Accident Witness Questions to prepare neutral prompts. For the agency records that can clarify what a crosswalk or lane displayed, see how to request signal timing and event logs. If the police report compresses a multi-impact collision, follow the guide to documenting a missing second impact.
