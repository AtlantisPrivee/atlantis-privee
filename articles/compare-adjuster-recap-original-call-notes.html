How to Compare an Adjuster’s Call Recap With Your Notes

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

You took notes during a call, then received an adjuster's recap that describes a different decision. Compare the two versions while the details are fresh. Focus on differences that could change an amount, a deadline, what you agreed to do or how the insurer understands the facts.

Identify the speaker and the exact difference

Keep the original notes, the recap and the call date. For each issue, compare the recap's exact words, what you recorded at the time and any document that supports your recollection. Mark whether your notes contain a direct quote or a paraphrase. If the call had several people, identify who said what as accurately as you can.

For example, a recap says, “You agreed the shop would absorb the difference.” Your notes say you agreed to ask the shop to compare rates. The shop's later email can establish that it reviewed the rates, even if it cannot establish what the adjuster said. Keep both facts visible. Washington's claim-file rule describes the level of documentation needed to reconstruct relevant events and dates in insurer files. Washington claim-file documentation rule

Correct the action the recap assigns to you

You could write, “In the July 8 recap, the second paragraph says I agreed the shop would absorb the difference. My notes show I agreed to ask for a rate breakdown. The shop sent that breakdown on July 9. Please confirm how you recorded my response and whether the additional labor charge is still under review.”

If an oral exchange cannot be verified, say which part you remember and ask for the version the insurer used. The NAIC recommends keeping dates, times and written records of insurer communications if you need to raise the issue with a regulator. NAIC guide to documenting a complaint

Keep both records linked to the follow-up

The claim statement comparison tool can help you place the recap and your notes side by side. Keep the sent correction and any acknowledgment with both originals. For a date in a recorded statement, attach the record that supports the corrected date. If the insurer sends a transcript correction for signature, check exactly what the signature would confirm.
