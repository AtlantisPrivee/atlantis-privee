The Same Lien Appears Twice on Your Settlement Statement

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A closing statement might list $2,400 under medical bills and another $2,400 under reimbursement. Identical numbers deserve a question. The entries could represent the same obligation, distinct obligations, or a reserve shown beside a payment. The way to tell is to follow each proposed deduction to the person being paid and the record that authorizes it.

Compare the payee, account and status

Take the two lines from the settlement statement and ask for the current document behind each. For a provider balance, match the billing entity, service dates and latest ledger. For a health-plan recovery demand, identify the plan or recovery administrator, its claim reference and the service lines in the demand. For a trust-account hold, identify the amount still being held and whether it has already reduced the amount payable to you.

An old lien notice and an updated payoff from the same creditor can describe one debt at different stages. Likewise, a provider balance and an insurer's demand can concern the same treatment while representing different amounts and different payees. Keep both on the worksheet until you can distinguish them. The Duplicate Settlement Deduction Checker can flag repeated labels and amounts for follow-up.

Reconcile the example to the dollar

Imagine a $30,000 settlement. The statement lists $10,000 in fees, $700 in costs, $2,400 as a hospital payment and $2,400 as a hospital reserve. Counting both entries leaves $14,500 for the client. Counting a single $2,400 deduction leaves $16,900. Ask the preparer to show whether the reserve is an additional amount to withhold or a note describing the same hospital item. A useful answer will identify who receives each dollar and which line belongs in the net calculation.

The question changes if a demand comes from Medicare. CMS distinguishes conditional payment information from the formal demand and describes a process for disputing charges unrelated to the case. Match the current demand and its underlying claims before treating a preliminary figure as the amount to disburse. CMS guide to Medicare recovery

Request a statement that shows what happened

Ask for a revised statement identifying each amount paid, still held or released. If you have counsel, ask which funds remain disputed and what document will resolve each line. As one state example, California's Rule 1.15 addresses accounting for client funds and keeping disputed funds separate until the dispute is resolved. The rule that applies to your lawyer depends on the jurisdiction. State Bar of California, Rule 1.15

For a bill that changed after health insurance reprocessed it, check the revised EOB workflow. When the payee is an employer health plan, trace the legal basis using the guide to dated plan amendments.

When the fee agreement changed during the claim, match the fee version to each payment. For a structured settlement, obtain a confirmed first payment date.
