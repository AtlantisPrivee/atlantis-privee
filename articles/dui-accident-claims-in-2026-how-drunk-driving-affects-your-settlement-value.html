DUI Accident Claims: Evidence, Damages, and Insurance

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 July 1, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A DUI arrest can give an insurance claim a clear investigative starting point. The police may have observations, test results, and witness accounts that are unavailable after many other crashes. You still need a separate record of how the crash happened, what it cost, and which policy applies. A criminal fine goes through the criminal process. A civil claim addresses losses and other remedies available under the applicable law. U.S. Courts guide to civil cases

Keep the criminal and civil records separate

Write down the crash report number, the investigating agency, and any criminal case number. Check whether a document is an initial crash report, a citation, a laboratory result, or the final court disposition. An arrest, a test result, and a conviction are different events. If timing matters, note when the collision happened, when an officer observed the driver, and when a sample was collected. NHTSA overview of alcohol-impaired driving

Look for evidence of the collision itself. The point of impact, witness vantage points, signal timing, and original video can bear on who caused which part of the crash. If a witness saw a vehicle cross the center line, record the witness's words and where they were standing. Ask a business or agency about preserving relevant footage before it is routinely overwritten. The Claim Evidence Organizer can keep each observation beside the record that supports it.

Separate losses from questions about punitive damages

Start with ordinary loss records. Match medical charges to the explanation of benefits and payment history. Track missed work with pay records and a dated work restriction. Keep repair estimates and receipts for actual transportation expenses. Review what goes into a car accident settlement before comparing offers with different terms. CMS guide to reading an explanation of benefits

Punitive damages depend on the jurisdiction, what the evidence shows, and applicable coverage. Florida provides a concrete example. Its intoxication provision makes two specified punitive-damages provisions inapplicable to a defendant meeting its impairment or alcohol-level conditions at the time of the act or omission. Whether a claimant can obtain punitive damages still requires analysis of the case. Florida Statutes § 768.736

Compare the written terms of an offer

Ask what policies and limits the adjuster evaluated, what the payment would resolve, and who would sign the release. A bodily injury release can have different consequences from a payment for vehicle damage. A criminal restitution order, if one exists, also deserves its own entry so that the same expense is accounted for accurately.

Keep an offer's gross amount, costs, and any repayment claim on separate lines. The Net Settlement Calculator shows the arithmetic under the figures you enter. The guide to settlement versus trial explains what to check alongside the number, including the scope of a release and the evidence still in dispute.
