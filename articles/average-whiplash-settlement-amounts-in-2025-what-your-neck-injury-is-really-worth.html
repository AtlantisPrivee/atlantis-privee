Whiplash Claims: Neck Injury Records and Settlement Factors

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 11, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Whiplash can occur when the head and neck move suddenly, as in a collision. Neck pain has multiple possible causes. A medical evaluation can document the symptoms, findings, and follow-up appropriate to the injury. If a crash also involved a head injury, symptoms such as repeated vomiting, seizures, or worsening headache that does not go away warrant immediate emergency care under CDC guidance. MedlinePlus overview of whiplash · CDC head-injury danger signs

Make the early and follow-up records agree on the dates

Record when the crash happened, when each symptom first appeared, and when you discussed it with a clinician. Keep separate entries for the collision report, exam, referral, therapy, and any written work restriction. A later clinician can explain whether the course of symptoms and findings support a diagnosis. Use the Medical Records Tracker to see which visit summaries or referrals are still missing. HHS guidance on access to health records

Where your work or activities changed, describe a particular task and date. For example, turning your head repeatedly on a job, carrying tools, or sitting through a commute may each present different difficulties. Keep contemporaneous notes and relevant work records. The lost wages guide explains how to distinguish missed work from paid leave.

Reconcile charges before discussing a settlement amount

A provider's bill, an EOB, and a payment receipt each answer a different question. For each service, record the date, provider, and claim reference. Add the plan's stated patient responsibility and payments already made, then compare the result with the provider's requested balance. If an EOB shows $180 due and you already paid $60 toward that service, the simple remaining amount is $120 under those assumptions. Ask for an explanation if the bill differs. CMS guide to reading a medical bill

Review the offer against the evidence

A diagnosis by itself does not supply a reliable settlement amount. Coverage, responsibility for the crash, documented treatment and wage effects, and any ongoing limitations can change the assessment. If an offer combines several losses, request a written breakdown and the release language. For noneconomic harm, California's civil jury instructions illustrate an evidence-based assessment without a fixed multiplier. California Civil Jury Instructions, CACI 3905A

Compare the offer with your records and confirmed deductions using the Net Settlement Calculator. The car accident settlement breakdown explains which amounts to keep separate. The pain and suffering guide shows how to record changes in day-to-day activities without turning them into a formula.
