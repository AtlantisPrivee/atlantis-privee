Insurance Bad Faith: How to Document Claim Handling

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 25, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A disagreement about the amount or coverage can require a close reading of the policy. A claim-handling concern raises additional questions about what the insurer knew, what it did, and when. State law determines the applicable duties, available remedies, and procedures. California's insurance department publishes claim-handling rules as one example. California fair claims settlement regulations

Identify the relationship and the issue

Start with the coverage and the parties. A first-party claim seeks benefits under coverage available to the claimant. A liability claim can involve an insurer's handling of a demand against its insured. Different obligations and procedures can apply. Record whose policy it is, which benefit or demand is in dispute, and which documents the insurer relied on. California auto insurance coverage guide

For a disputed delay, record when notice and supporting documents reached the insurer, what it asked for, and what response you received. For a disputed valuation, preserve the report, comparables, and any written reasons for adjustments. A missing document, an unexplained change of position, and a coverage disagreement each need a different question.

Use the timeline to test a specific allegation

Create one entry per communication. Include the sender, date received, attachment list, and response date. Use the Insurance Claim Contact Log and keep delivery proof with your documents. A short example: if the insurer says it still needs the July repair estimate, identify which version it requested, when you sent it, and the message confirming receipt. That record is more useful than saying the insurer delayed.

The Harvey case shows why a missing communication can matter even where a carrier tenders limits. The Florida decision addresses an older record. Florida's current statute provides a conditional tender rule for liability insurance bad-faith claims. The 90-day period in subsection (4) depends on actual notice with evidence sufficient to support the claim amount, and on tender of the lesser of the limits or amount demanded. Separate notice requirements can apply under subsection (3). Florida Statutes § 624.155

Match the next step to the problem

Ask for a written decision and the documents or policy provisions behind it. If you believe the insurer's conduct violates state requirements, the state insurance department can review a consumer complaint within its role. Preserve the underlying appeal or filing dates independently. The Insurance Complaint Organizer helps assemble a factual timeline, supporting records, and requested outcome. NAIC guide to filing a complaint

If the first issue is a written denial, follow the coverage denial and bad-faith guide to separate the policy question from the way the claim was handled.
