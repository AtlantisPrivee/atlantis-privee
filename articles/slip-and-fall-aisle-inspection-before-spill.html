The Slip-and-Fall Log Shows an Aisle Check Before the Spill

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A store's log shows an inspection at 2:00 p.m. You fell at 2:17. The useful question is what the check covered and what happened during those 17 minutes. A signed entry describes a check. A camera, witness or incident note may tell you when the spill appeared or was first reported.

Confirm what the inspection covered

Request the incident report and the inspection entries surrounding the fall, including any route, department or zone identifiers the store uses. Identify the exact shelf, aisle entrance or display. Compare a record for the whole department with one that specifies the affected patch of floor. Ask whether the logged time is when an employee reached the area or when the route was signed off.

Seek footage and statements for the interval before and after the event. Keep the timestamps and their sources. A witness may have seen an employee pass the area without seeing what the employee checked. A camera may show a spill begin in one place and spread into a walking path later. Record the difference.

Compare the chronology against the notice issue

Consider two example timelines. In the first, the 2:00 check covers the exact location and the spill is visible for the first time at 2:16. In the second, the 2:00 entry covers a different aisle and footage shows the substance at 1:48. The second record creates a different question about how long the condition was discoverable and what the store knew.

The California Supreme Court's Ortega v. Kmart opinion considered whether a failure to inspect within a reasonable time could support an inference about notice. It recognized that the inference can be drawn from the evidence in the circumstances. California's civil jury instructions discuss whether a dangerous condition existed long enough that reasonable care would have discovered it. California Supreme Court opinion in Ortega · California civil jury instructions, CACI No. 1011

Put each time beside its source

Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to track inspection, spill onset, first report and fall as separate events. For an event you cannot place precisely, record the earliest and latest plausible times from your records. A short timeline with the original log and any video is easier to review than a single assertion that the store checked the aisle.

When two recordings use different clocks, align shared events before comparing the times. For a separate example of what an older inspection record establishes, read the rented boom lift delivery guide.
