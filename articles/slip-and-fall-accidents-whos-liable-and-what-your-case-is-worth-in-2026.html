Slip-and-Fall Claims: Hazard, Notice, and Evidence

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 15, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

When you report a fall, identify the exact spot, time, surface, and condition. Ask how to obtain the incident report or its reference number. Document the scene if you can do so safely. Wide photographs can show the route and warnings. Close photographs can show the substance, tread, or change in elevation. Label the time and photographer so others can distinguish the incident from later conditions. California Civil Jury Instructions, premises liability series

Identify the rule that fits the condition

A spill in a grocery aisle is different from a loose step, recurring leak, or broken railing. In Florida, the law for a transitory foreign substance in a business establishment requires evidence that the business had actual or constructive knowledge of the condition and should have acted. The statute gives two ways circumstantial evidence can establish constructive knowledge: the condition existed long enough for the business to have discovered it with ordinary care, or it recurred regularly enough to be foreseeable. Florida Statutes § 768.0755

For a fixed defect, maintenance requests, prior repairs, or inspection records may answer a different question about who was responsible and what they knew. See the premises liability guide to identify the company or person with control of the specific area. Rules vary by state and type of property.

Build a timeline around the condition

Suppose an illustrative store incident occurred at 3:10 p.m. A photograph at 3:13 p.m. can show what the floor looked like afterward. A witness who saw liquid at 2:55 p.m. or video showing when it appeared can address duration. A cleaning log or employee report can address inspection or actual notice. Give each item its own time, source, and limits. If video may exist, ask promptly about preserving the relevant time window.

The Insurance Claim Timeline Builder can order the entries. Use the Accident Photo Checklist to identify views you still need. Keep the initial incident description, later photographs, witness observations, and medical records in their original forms.

Connect the injury and losses to your records

Keep the first evaluation, follow-up instructions, and records of changes in work or daily tasks. Match provider bills with the corresponding EOB and payments before adding costs. The lost wages guide explains how to compare missed shifts with actual pay. A claim review should consider the evidence of the hazard and the evidence of the injury separately.
