The EOB Shows Payment to You but the Provider Balance Stayed the Same

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An explanation of benefits, or EOB, shows a payment to the patient or subscriber. Your provider still shows a balance. Ask the insurer who the payee was and whether the payment was issued or delivered. Then ask the billing office how it expects to apply that money to the specific service.

Check the payee and payment status

Record the claim reference, provider, service date, amount, payee and payment date. Ask the insurer whether it issued a check or electronic payment, where it was sent and whether it was returned, stopped or reissued. If you filed a claim yourself, compare the submission and reimbursement instructions. CMS's sample EOB identifies the payee separately from the provider and patient balance fields. CMS sample explanation of benefits

For Medicare services, how the provider participates and whether it accepts assignment affects the way payment is handled. Medicare's guide explains the differences between providers that accept assignment, non-participating providers and providers that have opted out. For a private plan, confirm the payee and terms directly with that plan. Medicare guide to provider assignment

Work out the provider's current expected amount

Imagine an out-of-network claim shows a $180 payment made to you. The office's itemized bill still shows $260 because its ledger records only payments it received. Ask how much of the bill the provider expects after any applicable adjustments, which service the payment relates to and how it will credit any payment you send. Save the receipt and the new balance once it posts.

If you already paid the office, compare the date and amount of that payment with the plan reimbursement. Ask the billing office and plan to explain a discrepancy before forwarding a duplicate payment. CMS's bill guide shows how provider payments and the current balance appear on a statement. CMS medical bill guide

Track delivery and posting separately

Use the Insurance Claim Contact Log to record who confirmed the payee, what was received and what posted to the provider's account. If the carrier sent payment to the office and the bill still says pending, check the timing of the hospital posting. If the payment came from a second insurer, reconcile it with the primary payment first.
