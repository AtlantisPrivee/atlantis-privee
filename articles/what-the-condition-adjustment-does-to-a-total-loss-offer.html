What the Condition Adjustment Does to a Total Loss Offer

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

A total loss estimate can start with familiar numbers. The report lists cars like yours, their prices, and a value that seems to follow from those prices. Then a line called condition adjustment moves the number before the deductible even appears. The question is what that line actually compares. Ninth Circuit opinion in Lara

Two Washington policyholders challenged a recurring condition deduction in Lara v. First National Insurance Company of America. The Ninth Circuit's 2022 decision explains why a shared line in a computer-generated report did not establish that a class of owners had all been underpaid. Washington's October 2026 claims-handling update gives owners additional grounds to ask for the evidence behind certain condition deductions. Ninth Circuit opinion, pp. 5–11 · Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner, R 2025-05

One adjustment, two comparisons

The court described a process in which CCC assembled comparable vehicles for the insurer and adjusted for differences. A dealer car can be presented in better condition than a typical privately owned car. A downward adjustment accounted for that assumed difference. The condition of the owner's specific vehicle could then affect the amount in the other direction. Ninth Circuit opinion in Lara

Lara and another owner alleged that the way the standard deduction was presented failed to meet Washington's requirements. The court affirmed denial of class certification. Deciding injury in their proposed class would require individual work about vehicle value and the payment actually received. The ruling answers that class certification question. For the owner of a single car, the records still matter because they show whether a particular comparison and adjustment fit the vehicle. Lara, pp. 6–12

What the Washington report should show

Start with the report for your loss vehicle. The current text of WAC 284-30-392 calls for information collected during the initial inspection about its condition, equipment, and mileage, and the information the insurer used to value it. The comparable vehicles are to be listed with details such as the source, date, asking price, and sold price if available. Where the computerized-source provisions apply, weighting of vehicles used in an average must be explained. WAC 284-30-392

The updated rule separates two types of supporting records under its computerized-source provisions:

  • A comparable vehicle's condition reduced the payment. Ask the insurer for the photographs and documentation it used to determine that comparable's condition. The rule calls for providing that support upon request.
  • The insurer deducted value for the condition of the loss vehicle. The rule calls for providing supporting photographs and documentation for that condition determination.

Washington also requires explanations and dollar itemization for additions or deductions from actual cash value under its total loss settlement standards. Read the applicable version with the dates of your claim in mind. The amendments adopted under R 2025-05 took effect on October 18, 2026. WAC 284-30-391 · Adopted rule and effective date

A worked example with made-up numbers

Suppose a valuation report starts with an adjusted comparison average of $18,200. It lists a $900 deduction connected to the comparable vehicles' condition and another $350 for damage to your vehicle's interior that predated the crash. The resulting estimate is $16,950 before any deductible, taxes, or fees. A $500 policy deductible would put this simplified example at $16,450 before other applicable amounts.

Two different requests follow from those two deductions. For the $900 line, ask which vehicles it reflects and how their observed condition produced that amount. For the $350 line, ask which pre-loss condition the insurer documented and how it priced it. A dated photo showing the interior before the loss addresses the second line. A dealer listing and its photographs help check the first.

The arithmetic is simple. The value of the exercise is naming what would verify each input. If the report also has an adjustment for the same interior wear in a separate place, ask how the two entries relate. Seeing both lines is a reason to ask for an explanation. Whether they duplicate each other depends on what each one measures.

Review the report before comparing checks

First, confirm the loss vehicle's trim, drivetrain, major options, mileage, and pre-loss condition. Next, verify the date, configuration, location, and listing details of each comparable. Then copy the price adjustments into a short list with the amount, the vehicle it affects, and the insurer's explanation.

Keep valuation, deductible, taxes and fees, salvage, and loan payoff on separate lines when they appear. A lower amount sent to the owner can reflect a difference in valuation, a policy deduction, a payment to a lender, or some combination. A line-by-line comparison shows which part is actually disputed.

For qualifying first-party physical damage policies issued or renewed effective on or after January 1, 2026, Washington law requires a clause providing a right to appraisal for disputes over actual cash value and amount of loss. Read the clause before deciding how to challenge a valuation. Its process and costs are a separate consideration from asking for a corrected report. RCW 48.18.620

Match the deduction to the record it should have

When two adjustments both use the word “condition,” write down whether each describes the loss vehicle or a comparable. In Washington, the valuation-report rule identifies the information required for each and specifies support for certain condition reductions. For a quote from a comparable listing, save its date, options, and mileage so you can show exactly which fact you are checking. Washington valuation report rule

Use the Deduction Question Builder to ask for the source behind a line rather than requesting a new offer without explanation. The Texas towing case illustrates a different issue: a vehicle's value and loss of use can have separate evidence. The car accident settlement breakdown shows how to keep valuation, policy deductions, and other payments apart when you compare totals.

When the report leaves out a mobility conversion, start with the adaptive equipment valuation guide. If the disputed date comes from an appraisal award, separate its signature from the effective valuation date. For a repaired vehicle, compare diminished value using prices from the same side of the market.
