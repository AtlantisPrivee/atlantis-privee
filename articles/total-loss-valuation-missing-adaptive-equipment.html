The Total-Loss Valuation Omitted Dealer-Installed Adaptive Equipment

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A valuation report matches your van's VIN and mileage and compares it with ordinary vans on dealer lots. Your van has a lowered floor, a powered ramp and wheelchair securement points. The disagreement starts with the vehicle description. Ask the insurer to identify the modifications it recognized and how it valued each covered item.

Describe the equipment in terms an appraiser can match

Start with the conversion invoice and the vehicle VIN. Separate structural conversion work, permanently installed controls or lifts, and removable equipment. Include the conversion manufacturer, model and installation date, with labeled photographs and available maintenance records. A mobility dealer can help identify equipment from its markings and distinguish an integrated conversion from a removable device.

NHTSA's guide discusses vehicle modifications ranging from swivel seating to hand controls, along with the role of a qualified mobility dealer. Use the actual conversion and installation records to describe what was on your vehicle before the loss. NHTSA guide to adapting vehicles for people with disabilities

Find where the policy places the conversion

Ask whether the carrier considers the item part of the vehicle valuation, subject to a separate equipment endorsement, limited by a sublimit, or excluded by a particular term. Request that distinction in writing. An invoice shows what was installed and what it cost when purchased. An endorsement helps explain which costs may be covered. Current comparable vehicles help estimate what the equipped vehicle was worth.

Washington's total-loss regulation offers a specific example of why features matter. Its settlement rules call for appropriate adjustments for options, mileage or condition when determining comparability, and its valuation-report rule describes the information to include. Whether a particular modification falls within a policy's coverage is a separate question. WAC 284-30-391 · WAC 284-30-392

Submit comparable vans with the conversion described

In a sample request, two same-year vans list at $24,000. The first has a standard floor. The second has a ramp but a different conversion and a different mileage. A used mobility van priced at $32,500 can be informative if it matches the conversion, location and relevant date. Explain what is alike and what still needs an adjustment. Keep dealer details, the listing date and the equipment configuration with each lead.

Use the Claim Expense and Receipt Tracker to map invoice lines to the equipment shown in the photos. Ask for an itemized revised report or an explanation of the treatment of each item. If the report uses a later market date, check which date the appraiser actually valued. If a refinance has also complicated the payment, trace the check and lien separately.
