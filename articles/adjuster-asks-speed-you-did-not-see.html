What to Say When an Adjuster Asks About a Speed You Did Not See

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

The adjuster asks how fast another car was going. You saw it enter the intersection but could not measure its speed. Say what you observed and where your observation began. Explain the limits of your view, then provide the details you can support.

Describe the observation in sequence

Give your location, line of sight, the direction of travel and the first point where you saw the vehicle. Note whether a parked car or another obstacle blocked the earlier approach. Separate what you saw from what you heard from another witness or later read in a report. The National Institute of Justice's eyewitness evidence guide describes collecting and recording a witness's recollection. That distinction helps keep an insurance account clear too. NIJ guide to eyewitness evidence

A response could be, “I saw the blue car enter the intersection from the east when I was stopped at the line. A van blocked my view of the approach. I couldn't determine its speed. I saw it pass the crosswalk before the impact.” If you can describe an interval or landmark without measuring speed, give the detail and identify it as an observation.

Identify the source of any number

If you know the posted limit, label it as the limit. If an officer or expert estimated speed, give the source and the date of the estimate. A dashcam recording can preserve what happened and may support a later analysis if timing and other inputs are verified. NHTSA's Model Minimum Uniform Crash Criteria illustrates how crash data distinguish separate facts and how reporting fields can vary by jurisdiction. NHTSA crash data guidance

If the adjuster repeats a number you did not give, ask them to note the correction and identify the source. Preserve your original account and the relevant photo or recording without changing it.

Keep the observation with the supporting evidence

Use the accident witness questions tool to organize position, visibility and sequence. If a dispute turns on what fact the carrier accepts, ask it to identify the disputed facts and supporting records. If a transcript adds a number you didn't say, check exactly what a correction form would certify.
