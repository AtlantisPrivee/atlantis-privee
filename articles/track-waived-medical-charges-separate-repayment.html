How to Track Waived Medical Charges Separately From Repayment

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Your provider agreed to reduce a bill before your settlement was paid. Record the reduction as a change to the balance and the payoff as a separate movement of money. The closing statement should show the amount actually paid from your proceeds and the records supporting any remaining balance.

Distinguish the provider’s adjustment from the payer’s recovery

Ask for the latest itemized bill, any applicable explanation of benefits, and the written waiver or payoff agreement. Identify the service dates and account covered. Ask when the waiver takes effect, whether it depends on a payment by a certain date, and what record will show that the account was resolved. CMS's bill guide shows the difference between charges, payments and the amount currently due. CMS guide to reading a medical bill

If a health plan or Medicare seeks repayment for services it covered, keep that demand on its own row with its own payee and supporting record. CMS explains how Medicare tracks conditional payments and issues a demand when applicable. A provider's write-off may affect its bill while the separate repayment process concerns funds another entity paid. CMS Medicare recovery process

Use the ledger to identify how much cash left the settlement

In a hypothetical example, a provider asserts a $3,000 balance. A signed agreement waives $1,200 once the provider receives $1,800 by the specified date. After payment and posting, the account statement would show the $1,800 cash payment and the $1,200 adjustment. For the settlement cash ledger, the outgoing amount is $1,800. Keep the written reduction with the bill so the full $3,000 is accounted for.

If the next bill still shows $1,200, ask whether the payoff was posted to the correct account and whether the condition for applying the waiver was satisfied. Ask for a revised statement or a written explanation of a separate amount still being claimed.

Reconcile the bill and the disbursement side by side

Use the medical bill and EOB comparison tool for the provider account and the Net Settlement Calculator for the actual payment. If another bill remains after the lien was resolved, match the written resolution to the service dates and accounts. If a refund is expected after posting, check who receives it and when it can appear in the settlement accounting.
