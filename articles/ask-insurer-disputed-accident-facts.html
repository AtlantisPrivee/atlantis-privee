How to Ask an Insurer Which Accident Facts It Disputes

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An insurer says it disputes liability or disagrees with your version of the accident. Ask it to name the exact fact in question and explain how that fact affects its position. A disagreement about which lane a car occupied calls for a different response from a disagreement about when the damage happened.

Turn a broad objection into a question you can verify

Separate three issues. Ask what the insurer believes happened, what records it relied on, and what decision followed. If its decision covers several issues, ask for an itemized response. Describe any evidence you have by name and date so the adjuster can identify it in the claim file.

California's fair claims rule addresses written notices for third-party disputes over liability or damages and factual and legal grounds for first-party denials. Washington's unfair claims practices rule includes standards for reasonable investigation and for explaining the policy basis in relation to the facts or applicable law when denying a claim or offering a compromise settlement. Which standard applies depends on the claim and jurisdiction. California fair claims standard · Washington unfair claims practices rule

Send the evidence that answers the question

Suppose the insurer says it cannot confirm where the impact happened. You might write, “Is the issue the lane I occupied before contact, the point where the vehicles touched, or the place the cars stopped afterward? The scene diagram and the two photos taken before the cars moved are in upload R32. Please confirm which fact is disputed and whether you have reviewed those records.”

If the insurer identifies a conflicting statement, ask for the substance and what records it can provide. Describe which facts your evidence directly shows. A photo of the final position, for example, can support where a vehicle stopped. An earlier position may need a separate record or analysis.

Build a response that mirrors the dispute

Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to put the disputed fact beside the record that addresses it. Keep dates and references so the reviewer can find the originals. If the dispute concerns speed you could not see, state where your observations begin and end. If the decision was communicated by phone, request the written decision and the coverage it affects.
