Hit-and-Run Accidents: Reporting, Evidence, and Coverage

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

When the other driver leaves, the immediate tasks are safety and reporting. Call 911 if someone is injured or the scene poses a danger. If you can do so safely, record a partial plate, vehicle description, direction of travel, and time. Give those details to the responding agency and follow its instructions. California Department of Insurance crash guide

Build an identification record

A plate fragment, a distinctive mark, and a timestamp may be more useful together than separately. Ask witnesses what they observed in their own words and keep their contact details private. Save original dashcam or phone files with the device's date and time information. If a business camera may have captured the event, record the camera's location and ask promptly about preservation. Keep later copies and edits separate from originals. If someone walking was struck, see the pedestrian crash evidence guide for how to record the crossing and signal phase.

Report the event to the insurer as required by the policy. Record the report number, the agency, and the name of each person you contacted. Note if the officer prepared an incident report or if another agency is handling the investigation. The Insurance Claim Contact Log keeps the police record, claim number, and follow-up dates distinct. NAIC auto claim guide

If a tow ticket is your earliest record, use its pickup details to identify the operator, first lot and possible corroborating sources. How to document a hit-and-run without scene photos.

Review coverage by type of loss

Vehicle repairs and injuries may follow different routes. Collision coverage can apply to covered damage to your own vehicle, subject to the policy terms and deductible. Uninsured motorist bodily injury coverage is a separate question. Ask whether the definition of an uninsured or unidentified vehicle applies, what proof the policy requires, and whether notice or contact requirements apply where you live. California's consumer guide distinguishes uninsured motorist bodily injury from uninsured motorist property damage and explains that its property-damage coverage has identification restrictions. California auto insurance coverage guide

Request the declarations page and any endorsements that affect the loss. Write down each coverage's deductible and limit in a separate column. An adjuster's first answer may address only one coverage. If the insurer says the vehicle must be identified, ask which coverage that statement applies to and where the requirement appears in the policy. The guide to a denied claim shows how to turn that answer into a focused written request.

Keep transport expenses tied to dates

If you need a rental while a vehicle is inspected or repaired, ask what the policy covers per day, how long coverage lasts, and whether there is a total cap. Save the authorization, rental agreement, and return receipt. If your policy allows $35 per day for an eligible rental costing $50 per day, the daily gap is $15 before other terms, taxes, or limits. Use the Rental Car Coverage Gap Calculator with your written terms.

A hit-and-run claim still benefits from the same record of injuries and expenses as other collisions. The post-crash checklist and car settlement breakdown cover the next records to collect.
