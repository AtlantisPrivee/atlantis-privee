Injury Discovery Asks for an Export From a Deleted Social Media Account

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A discovery request asks for a social media export from an account you no longer use. First identify what happened to the account and when. An account that was deactivated, an account you requested to delete and an account you simply cannot access present different record problems. Keep any confirmation emails or messages showing the actual status.

Identify the accounts, dates and materials requested

Give the request and any court order to the lawyer handling the claim. List the platform, account identifier, relevant period and categories requested. Record the last time you could access the account and any deletion, deactivation or lockout notices. Preserve the files you already have, including older downloads, device copies, attachments and existing screenshots. Keep their source and date.

For an account you can access, see which export categories, dates and formats are available. Meta describes its account-center tools for downloading information across its apps. Platform capabilities vary. Keep the original archive and a record of what you requested and received. If access is unavailable, document what the service confirms and ask counsel how to address gaps or seek records through the appropriate process. Meta's information-access tools

Tie the response to the scope and preservation rules

In Forman v. Henkin, the New York Court of Appeals considered discovery from a deactivated Facebook account and linked the scope of discovery to relevance and the issues in the case. A particular request can be limited by date, content and purpose. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e) addresses electronically stored information that should have been preserved for litigation, was lost because of a failure to take reasonable preservation steps, and is unrecoverable through additional discovery. Check the rules and order in your own case. New York Court of Appeals opinion in Forman · U.S. Courts Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 37(e)

Make a record of what you can provide

Keep a source list separating an actual platform archive, locally saved copies and items you know only from a notice or recollection. Record unsuccessful access or export attempts with dates and relevant responses. Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to track categories and sources without pasting private messages into a shared file.
