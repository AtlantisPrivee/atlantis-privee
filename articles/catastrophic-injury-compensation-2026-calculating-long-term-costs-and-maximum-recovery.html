Catastrophic Injury Claims: Documenting Long-Term Care Costs

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 25, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A useful long-term cost estimate begins with a service and a reason. For example, a written therapy recommendation can establish the expected frequency, while a provider estimate can supply the unit price. Keep the recommendation, price, and assumed duration in separate columns. California's civil jury instruction on medical expenses offers one example of the distinction between past reasonable costs and future care reasonably certain to be needed. California Civil Jury Instructions, CACI 3903A

Separate present care from future needs

Group records into care already received, care recommended for a defined period, and needs that require further evaluation. Include the source and date for each entry. For home support, describe the task, hours per week, and what changed after the injury. For equipment, record the model, quote, expected maintenance, and the reason behind any replacement interval.

Future care after a serious brain injury can involve several disciplines, including rehabilitation and therapy matched to the person's needs. A clinician can describe the function or goal each service addresses. The brain injury record guide explains how to keep symptoms, rehabilitation, and work restrictions linked to specific dates. NINDS overview of traumatic brain injury and rehabilitation

Show the assumptions behind a low and high scenario

Suppose a written plan calls for two visits per week for 12 weeks. If two estimates are $120 and $155 per visit, the illustrative range is $2,880 to $3,720 for 24 visits. Record whether the quotes include evaluations or equipment. If the frequency changes after reassessment, update that assumption in its own row. The Medical Care Cost Planner compares unit prices and quantities, and the Household Help Cost Calculator converts weekly hours and rates into a separate subtotal.

Some costs may recur monthly and others only when equipment is replaced. Track timing before combining them. Add a source for every price and label which items still need confirmation. A long-term projection may require clinical, vocational, and financial evidence appropriate to the case.

Reconcile billed charges, balances, and future projections

For completed care, match bills with the corresponding EOB and payments. For proposed care, keep the quote and plan's coverage terms separate from actual out-of-pocket spending. A conditional payment or a reimbursement claim can affect how proceeds are distributed after a settlement. Keep that amount outside the projected care subtotal until you know what it represents. CMS guide to reading a medical bill · CMS beneficiary recovery process

Maintain separate worksheets for care, lost wages and earning capacity, and changes in daily function. A structured record makes it easier to see whether one service appears in two categories, whether a quote is out of date, or whether you still need a written recommendation.

If weekly therapy is replaced by a home program, price the future services that remain in the revised plan.
