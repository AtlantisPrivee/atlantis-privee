How to Correct an Approximate Date in a Recorded Accident Statement

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

During a recorded call you said the accident happened “around May 12.” Later you found a dated record showing May 10. Send a focused clarification with the source and the date of the call. Keeping the original estimate and the corrected information together lets the adjuster understand why the dates differ.

Identify the statement and the source of the new date

Locate the claim number, date of the recording and the question or passage you want to clarify. Request a copy or relevant excerpt if available. Identify the contemporaneous record that supports the date, such as a police report, dispatch record or original photo. Keep the original file and note where it came from. NIST's digital evidence guidance explains the value of preserving files and recording how they were obtained. NIST digital evidence preservation guidance

A notice might say, “On the June 3 recorded call I estimated the accident date as May 12 because I was speaking from memory. The police report dated May 10 identifies the collision as occurring on May 10. Please add this clarification and the attached report to the statement record.” Give a date range instead if the evidence only narrows the timing.

Ask the insurer to show where it applied the correction

Ask for acknowledgment that the note was added and whether the claim's loss date field needs an update. If the date affects the coverage period, a deadline or the link to another record, ask the adjuster to confirm the active date used in the decision. Washington's claim-file rule illustrates why records should allow relevant events and dates to be reconstructed. For eligible first-party requests, it also describes access to certain statements and other records, subject to exceptions. Washington claim-file documentation rule

Keep the date of your correction and the loss date in separate fields. If a second document repeats the earlier date, identify that document in the same message and ask how the insurer will associate your clarification with it.

Preserve both versions and the supporting document

Use the Insurance Claim Timeline Builder to record the date of the accident, the statement and the correction separately. If the insurer asks you to sign a correction, check which version it would certify. If the issue came from the adjuster's written recap, compare the recap with your contemporaneous notes.
