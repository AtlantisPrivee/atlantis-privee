What to Do After a Car Accident: A Practical Checklist

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 10, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

The first useful record starts with safety. Call 911 for injuries or a dangerous scene and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Move yourself or the vehicle only when you can do so safely and local rules permit it. Exchange the information required where you are, and obtain the responding agency's report number. California Department of Insurance crash guide

At the scene, capture what can change

Photograph the vehicle positions if safe, then the lanes, signals, road surface, visible damage, and nearby signs. Take a wide photo and a closer one of the same feature. Keep the original files. Ask witnesses for contact information and a brief account of where they were when they saw the crash. Record a partial plate or camera location while it is fresh if a driver leaves. See what to preserve after a hit-and-run for identification and coverage questions.

Write the event down while you remember it, separating what you saw from what another person told you. If you later obtain a report, add it with its own date and source. The Insurance Claim Timeline Builder sorts those entries and leaves the source visible beside each one. NAIC guidance on documenting an auto claim

Open the claim and save the coverage questions

Notify the appropriate insurer under the policy's reporting terms. Ask for the claim number, assigned adjuster, inspection process, and any forms they need. Check whether a state crash report is also required. A police report and an insurer's claim number serve different purposes. In California, the insurance department's guide describes a separate DMV report for certain crashes, including those involving an injury. California Department of Insurance reporting guidance

Before authorizing repairs, ask who can inspect the car, how the shop handles supplemental damage found after disassembly, and what paperwork confirms any rental allowance. Save the original estimate, revised estimates, and rental dates. If the insurer treats the car as a total loss, ask for the valuation report and the comparable vehicles used. The total-loss condition-adjustment case shows how to review one line that changes the offer.

If the report mentions suspected impairment, keep the incident report, any later test result, and the court disposition under separate dates. The DUI accident claim guide explains what each can establish.

Match care, charges, and payments

Seek medical care when needed and follow the clinician's instructions. Some concussion symptoms can appear hours or days after an injury. CDC lists danger signs that warrant emergency care, including a worsening headache that does not go away, repeated vomiting, and unusual confusion after a head injury. CDC concussion symptoms and danger signs

If neck symptoms affect your routine, the whiplash documentation guide shows how to connect each visit, work restriction, and bill to the relevant date.

Keep the itemized bill, explanation of benefits, and payments for the same service together. An EOB explains the plan's handling of the claim. A provider bill requests payment. If the balance differs after accounting for payments, ask the provider to identify the charge or adjustment causing the difference. The medical bill and EOB tool helps compare these amounts. CMS guide to reading your medical bill

Before resolving any part of the claim

Ask for the proposed payment and release in writing. Identify the losses it covers, whether it closes injury or property claims, and what expenses or repayments remain. If you need to compare a property offer and an injury offer, keep them in separate rows. The guide to car accident settlement amounts explains how to reconcile those lines before comparing the bottom numbers.
