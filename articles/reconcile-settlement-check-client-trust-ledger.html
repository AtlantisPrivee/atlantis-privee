How to Reconcile a Settlement Check With Your Trust Ledger

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Your settlement check is smaller than the gross payment on the insurer's letter. Ask for the client-specific trust accounting and compare it with the settlement statement. The statement explains the intended split. The ledger shows the dated transactions and the funds that remain on hold.

Identify the deposits that funded your case

Record each payer, gross amount, payment reference and date the money reached the firm. Keep different coverages on different rows. Ask the firm to explain any deposit that is still pending, a credit paid directly to you, or a payment that went to another authorized payee.

California's professional conduct rules require lawyers to keep records of funds and property held under Rule 1.15 and to provide appropriate written accountings. The State Bar's trust accounting handbook includes examples and explains recordkeeping practices. Ask for the accounting that applies to your case and the rules that govern it. California Rule 1.15 · California State Bar client trust accounting handbook

Compare each distribution with the money that moved

Consider a hypothetical $30,000 deposit. The ledger records $9,000 to the firm for its fee, $450 in reimbursed case costs, $2,550 paid to a health plan, $16,000 paid to the client and $2,000 still held for a separate disputed item. The distributions total $28,000. The remaining $2,000 reconciles the ledger to the deposit. For this example, the client's check is $16,000 and the possible future payment depends on how the held funds are resolved.

Check the fee against the agreement and each reimbursement against the supporting invoice or payoff. If a line was labeled payable in the closing statement but has no transaction yet, ask when it was scheduled and whether the remaining funds include it. If a provider received funds directly from the insurer, ask where the payment appears in the overall settlement accounting.

Ask about one unexplained difference at a time

Use the Net Settlement Calculator to lay out the gross amount and each actual deduction. Keep held funds and estimated entries clearly labeled. If part of the proceeds remains in trust, identify the disputed amount and what will release it. If a cost looks like it was previously paid, trace the original payment before subtracting it again.
