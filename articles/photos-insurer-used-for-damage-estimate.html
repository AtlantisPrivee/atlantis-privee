How to Ask Which Photos the Insurer Used for a Repair Estimate

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

You sent photos of your damaged car, but the estimate skips a panel that appears damaged in one of them. Ask the adjuster which files were used for that part of the estimate. A file name or a photo index gives you a better way to locate the gap than sending the entire album again.

Tie your question to the estimate line

Write down the claim number, estimate date, vehicle and damage area. Identify the adjacent line or part if the affected item is missing. Keep any wide views that orient the reader, close-ups that show the damage and later shop photos taken after disassembly. Texas's insurance regulator recommends documenting accident damage and vehicle identifiers with photos when you can safely do so. Texas Department of Insurance photo guidance

Ask whether the estimate was based on your upload, an appraiser's inspection, photos from the shop or a combination. In Washington, an insured's right to request portions of a first-party claim file at reasonable intervals can include photographs, subject to the rule's disclosure exceptions. Washington claim-file access rule

Show where the two records diverge

In a hypothetical rear impact, estimate E1 lists the bumper cover. Your wide view shows which corner was hit, and a shop close-up labeled S2-04 shows a cracked retainer visible after disassembly. You could write, “Which photos support the bumper line on E1? Can you confirm whether S2-04 was available to the reviewer and whether the shop should submit that retainer on a supplement?”

Preserve the original photos and use copies for arrows or crops. Label any image that shows the vehicle after a panel was removed so the reviewer can place it in sequence. Ask the shop to identify the part and whether the added damage affects its estimate.

Turn the answer into a specific next step

Record whether the reviewer confirms the image was received, confirms it was considered or wants a different angle. Use the Accident Photo Checklist to record the view, part and filename. If the shop already sent its supplement, follow up on the exact revision in the queue. If the insurer says the damage is unrelated to the loss, ask which fact or evidence supports that conclusion.
