What to Ask Before Signing a Recorded Statement Correction

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An insurer sends you a correction page after you flag an error in a recorded statement. Read the language next to the signature and ask for the full version the insurer would keep. A page that identifies one correction can also refer to other pages or contain additional terms.

Identify the exact text covered by the signature

Ask for the original transcript, the corrected page and, if available, the corresponding audio excerpt. Record the recording date, page, line and question. Say whether the transcript captured the wrong word or whether you gave an answer that you now need to clarify. Those are different entries in a record.

For example, the transcript might say “I saw him cross before I moved” when the recording says “I saw him cross after I moved.” In another case, the transcript may be accurate but a timestamp gives you a better date. The first question is whether the transcript matches the audio. The second is how the insurer attaches your later clarification to the original answer. Washington's first-party claim-file rule gives eligible insureds a route to ask for statements and related records, subject to its exceptions. Washington claim-file access rule

Ask what the rest of the form does

Read the document as a whole, including referenced pages. If it refers to a certification, authorization or release, ask the insurer to specify its effect and whether the correction can be made on a separate form. California Civil Code § 1641 provides an example of a state rule that reads the parts of a contract together. For a form affecting substantive rights, confirm the applicable law and wording before signing. California rule on reading a contract as a whole

A request might read, “Please confirm whether the signature applies only to the change on page 3, line 12, or to the complete statement and any attached terms. Please send the complete corrected version you intend to keep.”

Save the version that actually goes into the file

Use the claim statement comparison tool to mark the changed words. Save the signed copy, attachments and delivery confirmation together if you send it. For a factual date correction, link the supporting record to the specific answer. If the issue began with a recap rather than a transcript, compare the recap against your original notes.
