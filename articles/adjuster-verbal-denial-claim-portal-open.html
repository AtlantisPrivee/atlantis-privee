The Adjuster Said Your Claim Was Denied but the Portal Is Open

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An adjuster says your claim was denied. When you log in, the portal still says “open.” Ask the insurer to identify the coverage, expense or request it decided and to give you the dated decision. A portal can show a status for the whole file while the phone conversation concerns one part of it.

Find out what the denial covers

Write down who called, when they called and the wording you remember. Use the policy and claim number to ask whether the decision concerns coverage, responsibility for the accident, one item on an estimate or another part of the claim. If property damage and rental are both under review, ask for the status of each.

California's fair claims regulation requires written denials or rejections of covered first-party claims with the applicable factual and legal bases, and addresses written denials, rejections or disputes over liability or damages for third-party claims. The regulation contains specific exceptions and qualifications. Washington has its own standards for first-party acceptance and denial, including written denials in the circumstances described in its rule. California denial standards · Washington claim settlement standards

Ask for the record you can act on

For example, an adjuster says “that was denied” after discussing a rental charge. The portal still displays a property-damage claim and an inspection appointment. You could write, “On July 8 I understood that the June 22 rental charge was denied. Please confirm whether the decision applies to that charge, the entire rental benefit or any other portion of claim 2719. Please send the dated decision and identify the provision or facts you relied on.”

Ask how to provide additional information or request review, and record any deadlines the insurer identifies. If a deadline is uncertain, check the policy, the dated correspondence and the applicable rules independently of the portal display. Save a dated screenshot of the status if it explains the mismatch.

Keep a record of the answer

Use the Insurance Claim Contact Log for the call, the portal status and the eventual written response. If the insurer's decision relies on a point of disagreement about the crash, ask the reviewer to identify the specific fact it disputes. If the call came from a new adjuster, rebuild the handoff from the earlier records.
