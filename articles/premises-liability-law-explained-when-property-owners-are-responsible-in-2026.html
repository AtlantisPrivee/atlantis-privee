Premises Liability: Who Controlled the Property and the Hazard?

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 25, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A property injury can raise questions about ownership, control, maintenance, and notice. The owner, tenant, property manager, or contractor may have had different responsibilities. Start with the exact location and the task that could have prevented the injury. California's premises instructions, for example, ask about ownership or control, reasonable care, harm, and causation. The applicable state's rules determine the elements for a particular claim. California Civil Jury Instructions, CACI 1000 and 1001

Identify who controlled the specific area

Describe the area precisely. “North entrance, outside the automatic doors” is more useful than the name of a shopping center. Ask who inspected the area, who could make a repair or put up a warning, and whether the tenant or a common-area manager was responsible. A deed, lease, work order, or service contract can point to different people. A name on a sign alone does not explain control of that area.

The law may consider why someone was on the property, whether the condition was open and obvious, and what reasonable care required under the circumstances. A single national chart of visitor categories can obscure important state differences. California's instructions illustrate a duty analysis focused on the circumstances. California Civil Jury Instructions, premises liability series

Connect the condition to notice and the injury

Photograph the condition and its surroundings when it is safe. Include measurements or context where relevant. Record who observed the hazard, whether anyone reported it before the incident, and what the inspection or repair records say. For a recurring leak, earlier work orders or repeated complaints can address a different question from a photo taken after a fall.

Florida's statute for a slip on a transitory foreign substance in a business establishment specifically addresses actual or constructive knowledge. Other premises claims can turn on different duties and evidence. See the slip-and-fall evidence guide for a worked example of what can establish notice in that setting. Florida Statutes § 768.0755

If an animal caused the injury, keep the animal owner's conduct separate from any question about who controlled a gate, fence, or common area. The dog bite guide compares the state rules that can govern the animal owner's liability.

Organize what you know and what remains open

Make a list of the condition, who controlled it, what the relevant parties knew, how the injury happened, and what supports each fact. The Claim Evidence Organizer can keep records and open questions side by side. A photo may show a broken tread. An inspection schedule may show who should have checked it. Neither substitutes for a record connecting the condition to the injury.

If a public entity controls the site, check the filing and notice deadline guide early. Some jurisdictions require a government claim before a lawsuit. California's self-help court guide describes the separate claim procedure and timing. California Courts government claim guidance
