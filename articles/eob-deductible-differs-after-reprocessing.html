Your EOB Deductible and Provider Bill Differ After Reprocessing

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

A reprocessed explanation of benefits assigns $100 to the deductible. The provider shows a different balance. Look first at the difference between the deductible portion and the total current patient share. A provider's statement may combine that share with other patient amounts, prior credits or separate services.

Locate the deductible on the active claim

Ask the plan to confirm the active determination, service date, claim line and amount assigned to the deductible. Identify any other cost-sharing amounts on the same line. Ask what happened to the deductible entry from the earlier determination, particularly if the plan reversed it. HealthCare.gov explains the deductible and notes that different plan designs can have separate deductibles or cover certain services before the deductible is met. HealthCare.gov deductible definition

Keep the plan's deductible tracker separate from the individual claim. The tracker may show the balance for the year or for a family, while the EOB assigns a specific amount for one service. If the plan reprocessed several claims, ask how it adjusted the running totals and which policy period the service belongs to.

Reconcile the bill line with the current patient amount

Suppose the active EOB lists $100 deductible and $40 coinsurance, for $140 patient responsibility. You already paid $25 to the provider for that encounter. If that credit is still on the account, the unpaid share in this example is $115. If the statement reads $215, ask whether the provider added the deductible twice or included another charge. Request a line-by-line explanation instead of relying on the label on the bill.

CMS explains that an EOB's patient balance may include amounts the patient has already paid. Ask the billing office for the posted payments, applied credits and current balance for that service. CMS EOB guide · CMS medical bill guide

Send the question with the exact amounts

Put the active EOB amounts and the provider's itemized balance into the medical bill and EOB comparison tool. If the difference began with a provider correction, compare the two claim decisions and the changes to each line. If the number is on an installment portal, check which charges the payment plan actually covers.
