Your Medical Bill Still Shows a Balance After a Revised EOB

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A revised explanation of benefits (EOB) can change the amount your health plan assigns to you while the hospital still displays an old balance. Reconcile those two records with your payment receipt before treating the number on either screen as the amount still due. The insurer explains how it processed the claim. The provider's ledger shows what it actually charged and collected. CMS guide to reading an EOB

Find the version the provider posted

Open the original and revised EOBs together. Find the service date, provider, claim reference and service line. Look for a replacement or adjustment indicator and record the date the plan processed the revision. If the plan issued a new reference number, ask it to identify which earlier claim that number replaces.

Now ask the provider for the current itemized bill and the existing payment history for that encounter. A balance-only screen hides the useful columns. You want the dates and amounts of patient payments, insurer payments, adjustments and any reversal or transfer between accounts. HHS identifies billing, payment and claims records as examples of records that can be accessible under HIPAA when the rule applies. HHS guidance on access to billing records

Follow the money through one example

Suppose you paid $150 at check-in. The first EOB assigned you $340, and the provider sent a bill for $190 after crediting the deposit. The revised EOB assigns you $220. If the provider posts the revision and the $150 remains credited to that same encounter, the remaining amount in this example is $70. If the portal shows $220, check where the deposit went. If it still shows $190, ask whether the provider has posted the revised insurer remittance.

A single visit can produce separate hospital and physician bills. Match the receipt to the same billing entity before subtracting a payment. Enter the insurer's current patient responsibility and the payment actually credited to the account in the Medical Bill and EOB Reconciler.

Ask for the corrected balance in writing

I paid $150 on May 4. The revised EOB for the May 4 encounter assigns $220 to me. Please confirm the revised claim was posted, identify the ledger entry for my payment and send an itemized statement showing the current balance.

Send it through the provider's secure channel. If billing or collection notices continue while the provider researches the payment, ask for written confirmation of the account's status and keep the notices. For a balance involving an auto-insurance payment, see how to trace MedPay through a hospital ledger. If the health plan actually denied a service, start with the imaging authorization guide.
