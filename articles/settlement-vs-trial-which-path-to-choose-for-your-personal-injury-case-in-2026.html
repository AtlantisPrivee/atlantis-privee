Settlement vs. Trial: Compare the Terms, Costs, and Risks

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A settlement is an agreement on terms. A trial asks a judge or jury to resolve issues the parties have not resolved. Settlement discussions can occur before or during litigation, and mediation may help the parties explore an agreement. The U.S. Courts civil case guide describes discovery and trial as distinct stages, with opportunities to resolve the dispute along the way. U.S. Courts overview of civil cases

Review the offer as a document

Ask for the proposed agreement and payment terms. Identify who pays, who is released, which claims are covered, and when payment is due. Check whether the offer separates property and injury claims, and what happens to outstanding bills or asserted repayment rights. Ask how the fee agreement handles case expenses and the basis for calculating the fee. California State Bar guidance on fees and costs

Use the Compare Net Settlement Offers tool for the numbers. In an illustrative example, a $30,000 offer with $8,000 in combined deductions leaves $22,000. A $32,000 offer with $12,000 in deductions leaves $20,000. The better comparison still depends on the release, timing, certainty of payment, and whether each deduction is supported. If Medicare is involved, see how its recovery process can affect settlement proceeds. CMS beneficiary recovery process

Make a list of what would have to be proved

For each disputed issue, write the evidence that supports your position and the evidence that cuts against it. Label what remains uncertain. A witness may explain what happened before the collision, while a clinician may address causation or treatment. The Claim Evidence Organizer helps keep those questions distinct.

Trial preparation can involve experts, depositions, document exchanges, and motions. A verdict may still be followed by post-trial proceedings or an appeal, and collection can be a separate practical issue. Ask counsel how fees, expenses, appeals, and collection would be handled under your actual agreement. U.S. Courts overview of civil proceedings

When several clients share one invoice, compare each claimant's gross share and allocated cost. Once funds are distributed, reconcile the check to the dated client ledger.

Compare scenarios without turning them into predictions

A hypothetical favorable and unfavorable outcome can make the assumptions visible. The Break-Even Probability Calculator compares arithmetic scenarios you enter. It supplies no probability for the dispute. A decision can also turn on factors that fit poorly into one dollar amount, such as uncertain timing or whether the release covers a separate claim.

Keep the settlement timeline guide next to the written offer. Review the next procedural deadline separately from how long the offer remains open. A deadline for accepting an offer and a deadline for filing a claim can have different sources and consequences. California Courts guide to filing deadlines
