How to Track Repeated Requests to Resend a Claim Attachment

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

The insurer asks for the same attachment a third time. Find your first confirmation and send a dated record that distinguishes delivery, successful opening and assignment to the claim. Each of those can fail at a different point. Your next message should help the insurer tell you which one happened.

Keep a receipt for each transfer

Create one row per attempt with the date, channel, recipient, file name, version and confirmation number. If a portal lists the upload, keep a screenshot showing its status and date. Record a rejection notice or file-size error beside the attempt. For a sensitive document, use the verified secure route the insurer gives you.

NIST's guidance on digital evidence describes preserving files and documenting their origin and handling. In an ordinary claim, keeping the file you actually sent and its delivery receipt gives you a stable record to compare with later copies. The NAIC also recommends retaining the correspondence and dates when documenting a complaint. NIST guidance on digital evidence preservation · NAIC complaint preparation guide

Ask which step needs to be repeated

Here is a hypothetical example. The first portal upload of Invoice-June12.pdf gave receipt U18. A second upload gave U44. The adjuster can see U44 but says the attachment is password protected. Instead of uploading the same copy again, confirm an approved way to provide a readable version. Keep it under a distinct filename and note which earlier upload it replaces.

You could ask, “Can you confirm whether Invoice-June12.pdf was received, opens successfully and is attached to the June 12 service under claim 3149? The upload references are U18 and U44. Please tell me if a different format or upload route is needed.”

When a third party sent the file, ask that sender for its receipt. Give the reviewer the sender's name and submission date so it can search under the right account or contact.

Mark the issue closed when the reviewer confirms the file

Use the Insurance Claim Contact Log to record the insurer's response and which version is active. If someone else took over the file, use the original receipt to orient the new adjuster. If the insurer is missing several different records, send one status list for all open documents and attach only the files it has identified.
