A Carrier's Safety Rating Changed After a Freight Broker Tendered a Load

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A SAFER report pulled after a truck crash shows a carrier's current rating. To examine what information was available when a broker selected the carrier, use the carrier's USDOT number and preserve dated records. The tender, any later reassignment, the agency notice and the rating's effect can occur on different days.

Start with the carrier on the shipment

Compare the USDOT number on the broker's carrier agreement or onboarding record with the carrier shown on the tender, dispatch records and bill of lading. A similar name on a current search result is weak evidence of identity. If a load changed hands, mark the time and source of each change. The federal broker records rule describes transaction records kept by brokers and provides review access to parties to a brokered transaction. Whether a person can obtain other records depends on the requester and applicable process. Federal broker transaction records rule

Put each safety record at the right point in time

Keep a copy of the rating result and the date you retrieved it. Separate a proposed safety determination, the agency's notice and any final or effective status. Federal regulations address notice and the effect of an unsatisfactory rating, including timing rules that can differ by carrier type. The actual notices, any changes to them and applicable orders matter to the timeline. Federal motor-carrier safety fitness rules

The Safety Measurement System is a separate FMCSA source. A prioritization indicator or inspection history is a different record from a formal safety rating. Note which dataset supplied each observation and how current it was. FMCSA SAFER Company Snapshot · FMCSA Safety Measurement System explanation

Compare what was known at tender and before pickup

In a hypothetical file, the broker tenders a load on Monday. The carrier receives a proposed rating notice on Wednesday, collects the load on Friday and has an updated public rating the next month. Friday's question is whether any new notice or status reached the relevant parties before pickup. Monday's question is what they could see or had received when making the initial assignment. A copy pulled the next month cannot answer either on its own.

Ask for the dated onboarding checks, rechecks, tender acceptance, any substitution records and any notice that was sent before dispatch. Use the Insurance Claim Timeline Builder to attach each observation to its record and retrieval time. For the liability context, read the freight broker case study. If the chronology also uses recorded footage, check the video clock separately.
