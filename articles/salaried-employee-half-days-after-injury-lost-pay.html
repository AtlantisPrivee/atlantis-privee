You Returned to Salaried Work for Half Days After an Injury

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

A medical restriction limits your return to four hours a day. Your salary may stay the same while you use accrued leave, or the pay statement may show unpaid time. Compare what was scheduled, what you worked, what you were paid and which leave account changed. Keep those measures separate in the claim record.

Ask payroll to explain the affected pay periods

Collect the written restriction, return-to-work schedule, gross pay stubs and leave statements before, during and after the half days. Ask the employer to confirm your usual hours, hours worked, paid leave used, unpaid leave and any changes to base salary or recurring compensation. Use gross amounts consistently and flag a benefit or withholding change if it alters your take-home comparison.

For employees covered by the federal salary-basis exemption rules, federal regulations specify when salary deductions may be made, including an exception for unpaid FMLA leave. The FMLA regulations separately address how reduced-schedule leave is counted when applicable. Whether a deduction was permitted depends on the worker's classification, leave and governing law. 29 CFR § 541.602 on the salary basis · 29 CFR § 825.205 on reduced-schedule FMLA leave

Compare pay and leave as separate amounts

In an example, usual gross pay is $1,200 per week. You work half days for two weeks and receive $2,400 total. Payroll records 40 hours of paid leave used. The pay comparison shows no reduction in gross salary in that period. The leave ledger shows 40 hours used. Ask the reviewer how the applicable claim treats used leave and preserve the employer's balance records.

If gross pay actually fell, compare the amount paid with the documented usual amount for the same period. Show any paid leave or wage-replacement benefit on its own line and record the dates it covers. A change in net deposit can also reflect withholding, so explain discrepancies using the detailed pay record.

Keep a dated record of each phase

Use the Phased Return-to-Work Planner to record restrictions, hours and changes in schedule. If the reduction also changed shift premiums, see how to calculate the missed differential separately. If your return followed a California work injury, keep the schedule with the record of when the DWC-1 was returned.
