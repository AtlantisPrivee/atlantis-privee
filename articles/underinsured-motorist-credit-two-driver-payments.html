An Underinsured Motorist Claim After Two Drivers Paid

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Two insurers pay after a multi-vehicle crash. Your underinsured motorist carrier then lists a credit on its worksheet. To check it, follow each dollar to the driver, coverage and injury or property claim it resolved. Then ask which law and policy provision the carrier used to decide whether that amount counts toward a credit.

Build the payment record by driver

For each insurer, keep the declarations or written limits information if available, the offer, signed release and payment confirmation. Record the driver, insurer, claim number, amount, payee, date and coverage. Flag any amount paid to another claimant from the same limits. Label bodily injury, property damage and medical payments separately.

In an example, Driver A's carrier pays $22,000 for bodily injury. Driver B's carrier pays $12,000 for bodily injury and issues a separate $4,000 property check. Your record should show $34,000 in bodily injury proceeds and a separate $4,000 property payment. If a worksheet shows $38,000 on one injury credit line, ask the adjuster to explain the allocation and reconcile it to the documents.

Find the rule on the policy that responds

Virginia provides one example of why the policy election matters. Its statute generally provides for UIM payment without a credit for the applicable liability coverage available for payment, subject to an election signed by a named insured to reduce UIM payments. The same statute defines available liability coverage by reference to coverage applicable to the injured person's claim after payments on other claims from the same occurrence. Ask for the actual endorsement and any election the insurer relies on. Virginia Code § 38.2-2206 · Virginia's statutory election notice

Ask the insurer to show the applicable injury or property limit, the basis for each credit and the order of calculation if more than one UIM policy is involved. If the payment was shared with other injured people, request the record supporting the amount allocated to you. Check any policy consent or notice requirements before signing a release that could affect another claim.

Ask for a worksheet you can reproduce

Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to connect each credit on the worksheet to a payment document or policy provision. Keep the final calculation and the carrier's earlier versions. The amount recoverable depends on the coverage, damages and governing law.

If coverage itself is disputed because you were using a fleet car, start with the fleet-car regular-use guide. If a liability release has a mismatched date, review the draft release before relying on it.
