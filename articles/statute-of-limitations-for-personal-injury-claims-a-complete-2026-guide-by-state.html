Personal Injury Filing Deadlines: How to Check the Right Clock

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A statute of limitations gives a time limit for bringing a legal action. A government notice, insurance notice, health-plan appeal, and court filing can each have their own deadlines. Start by identifying the jurisdiction, claim, defendant, and the event that starts each clock. Filing requirements and exceptions can depend on facts such as when a claim accrued and which version of the law applies. California Courts guide to filing deadlines

Compare statutory examples before using a state number

  • California. Many personal injury actions have a two-year limit. The courts describe separate periods for some claims, including medical malpractice. California Courts deadline guide
  • Texas. The general statute lists two years from accrual for personal injury. It separately addresses actions for injuries resulting in death. Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.003
  • New York. CPLR 214 generally lists three years for injury to person, subject to exceptions. Wrongful death has a separate statute. New York CPLR 214 · EPTL § 5-4.1
  • Florida. The current statute generally lists two years for actions founded on negligence. Verify which law applies to the date the claim accrued. Florida Statutes § 95.11

These examples illustrate the need to match the claim to its rule. A bodily injury, medical malpractice, contract, or wrongful death claim may have a different period in the same state. Discovery, minority, statutes of repose, or special notice provisions can affect the analysis. Verify any exception with the actual statute and relevant facts. See the medical malpractice records and deadlines guide and wrongful death guide.

Check public defendants separately

California courts explain that many injury claims against a government agency require submitting an initial claim within six months. New York's general municipal notice statute ordinarily requires notice within 90 days for covered tort claims, with specific provisions including the rule for wrongful death. Under the Federal Tort Claims Act, the statutory scheme generally includes presentment to the appropriate agency within two years of accrual and a six-month period after mailing of a final denial for bringing suit. Identify the agency and procedure before using any of these examples. California government claim guide · New York General Municipal Law § 50-e · 28 U.S.C. §§ 2401 and 2675

Keep a record of confirmed dates and sources

Write down the injury date, dates of discovery or notice that may matter, which entity received any filing, and proof of delivery. For a confirmed deadline, include the rule or professional who confirmed it. The Insurance Claim Date and Reminder Planner records dates you supply and exports them to a calendar. It supplies no legal deadline. The settlement timeline guide explains how to keep negotiation milestones separate from formal filing and notice dates.

If an initial California government claim went to the wrong agency, trace actual receipt by the proper office.
