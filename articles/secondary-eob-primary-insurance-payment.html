How to Compare a Secondary EOB With the Primary Payment

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Your primary plan paid part of a bill. The secondary plan issued its own explanation of benefits, or EOB. Start with the same date and service line on both notices. The useful question is how the secondary plan accounted for the first payment when it reached its own decision.

Identify the order and the matching line

Record the provider, service date, primary claim number and secondary claim number. Ask both plans which coverage was primary for that service and whether the second plan received the first EOB or an electronic crossover. If the first plan has since reprocessed the claim, ask which version the secondary plan used.

Medicare's coordination guide explains the primary and secondary payer sequence for people with Medicare and other coverage. The order and benefits vary with the type of coverage and the circumstances. For two other plans, ask for the coordination rules that govern your claim. Medicare's coordination of benefits guide

Track the amount each plan actually assigned

On the primary notice, record the allowed amount, payment and responsibility. On the secondary notice, find the reported primary payment, any secondary adjustment, the secondary payment and the final amount assigned to you if shown. If the secondary notice records a different primary payment, ask the billing office which primary decision it submitted.

For example, the primary plan pays $100 and lists $50 in patient responsibility. If the secondary plan accounts for that $100 and pays $30 against the eligible remainder, a provider ledger reflecting both payments might show $20 still due for that line. Verify the applicable amounts before treating that example as your bill. CMS's EOB guide explains how plan payments and the patient balance are presented. CMS guide to reading an EOB

Get the provider's current balance

Use the medical bill and EOB comparison tool with the current notices and the provider's posted credits. Ask whether both decisions were applied to the same account and whether a payment you made earlier is still credited. Save the date each office confirmed the result.

If the two notices could belong to different services, classify the EOBs before comparing the totals. If the primary plan revised the same reference twice, verify which decision the secondary payer received.
