Wrongful Death Claims: Who Can File and What to Document

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 17, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

After a death, relatives may receive requests from insurers, medical providers, or agencies before anyone has established who may act for the estate. Keep those requests, the relevant policies, and any appointment papers together. Eligibility to receive a share of a recovery and authority to file the claim can be different questions. New York's wrongful death statute illustrates that distinction by giving the right to maintain an action to a duly appointed personal representative in the circumstances it describes. New York EPTL § 5-4.1

Identify the type of claim and the person who may act

A wrongful death action may address losses suffered by eligible beneficiaries. A separate survival provision can allow a claim that belonged to the decedent to continue through the estate. Each state's law determines who acts, who may benefit, and which damages are available. New York has separate provisions for wrongful death and the survival of certain causes of action. New York EPTL §§ 5-4.1 and 11-3.2

For the person reviewing the file, collect the death certificate when available, documents identifying any appointed representative, and the underlying incident record. If the death followed a hospital stay, ask counsel who may request the medical records and what proof of authority is required. HHS describes how a personal representative's authority relates to access under HIPAA. HHS guidance on personal representatives

Organize losses under the applicable rule

List each claimed loss with the document that supports it. Income records, records of support, and invoices for medical or funeral costs can answer different questions. New York's damages provision addresses pecuniary injuries to eligible beneficiaries and specified expenses. Other jurisdictions define recoverable losses differently. Avoid applying a general settlement range to a family or assuming every form of loss is recoverable. New York EPTL § 5-4.3

For a recurring contribution, preserve the actual dates and amounts. For an expense, save the invoice and the record of payment separately. The Claim Expense and Receipt Tracker can help sort invoices, payments, and reimbursements under your own labels.

Confirm each deadline independently

New York generally requires the wrongful death action within two years after death, subject to the statute's exceptions. Government defendants can involve an additional notice requirement. New York's municipal notice statute includes a specific rule for the timing of notice in wrongful death actions. Verify both the responsible entity and the triggering date before relying on either period. New York EPTL § 5-4.1 · New York General Municipal Law § 50-e

Use the filing deadlines guide to distinguish notice from suit. If a consultation is next, the first attorney consultation guide gives a way to organize the records and the questions about representation, fees, and next dates.

For a claim subject to New York's municipal notice rule, check the estate appointment and service dates separately.
