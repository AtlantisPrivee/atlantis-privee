The Insurer Used a Comparable Car That Had Already Sold

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

You call the dealer named on a total-loss report and learn that the car sold weeks earlier. The useful follow-up is to find out when it was actually offered, what price the insurer used and how the listing was used. A vehicle offered as a replacement and a historical market observation serve different roles.

Get the dates that sit behind the row

Request the complete valuation report and identify the vehicle by VIN or stock number. Record the loss date, the valuation's effective date and the listing capture date. Ask the dealer to confirm when the car was available, when it sold or was removed and which price the dealer advertised. Ask the insurer whether the price shown is a listing amount, a reported sale amount or an adjusted value from the valuation vendor.

If the dealer confirms only that the car is gone today, ask a narrower question about its status on the date shown in the report. Preserve the written response and any original listing that still exists. A reused photo or a reposted ad can make the capture date look current after the actual availability ended.

Check the valuation method and market window

In Washington, an insurer using the replacement method must offer a comparable motor vehicle available for inspection within a reasonable distance. Cash valuations use a different framework. Washington's total-loss rule allows certain historical comparables, including vehicles available within 90 calendar days of the loss in the rule's itemized settlement requirements. The rule separately addresses the data a computerized source must use. Washington total-loss settlement rule

Consider a Washington loss on November 1. A car the dealer sold on October 20 could still be relevant to a cash valuation if the data and the vehicle meet the applicable criteria. A January sale from before that November loss raises a different question. Ask the insurer which observation it used and where it fits in the stated market window. Washington's valuation-report rule describes details the claimant can use to verify the listed vehicles and adjustments. Washington valuation-report requirements

Request the correction that the evidence supports

Send the VIN, the challenged row, the dealer's dated response and any better comparables with their availability and price. Ask the insurer to confirm the data, explain why it qualifies or provide an itemized revised valuation. The Claim Evidence Organizer can pair each disputed entry with its source.

If the report uses a different effective date, read how to check the date in a total-loss appraisal. If your rental cutoff depends on the settlement timeline, keep it separate using the total-loss rental guide.
