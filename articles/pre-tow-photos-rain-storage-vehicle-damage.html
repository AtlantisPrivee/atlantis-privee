Photograph Crash Damage Before Rain and Tow Yard Storage Change It

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A wet fender can look different from the same fender under a dry shop light. Later photos may also show surface changes, dirt or a different position. For an insurance file, organize images by when and where they were taken so a reviewer can compare the same area across conditions.

Get a baseline when it is safe

Leave traffic lanes and follow the instructions of emergency responders. If you can photograph the car from a safe position before it is moved, start with a wide image showing the vehicle and its location. Then capture an angle that shows where the damaged panel sits and a closer view of what is visible. Note which areas you could not safely see. If the first safe view is at the tow destination, record that as the first image rather than describing it as a scene photo. California insurance department's accident guide

Use the Accident Photo Checklist to track the sides and details you captured. Keep the camera originals and use copies for markup, cropping or sharing. Preserve the source and date of each file. NIST's digital evidence guidance describes preserving data and documenting its handling so that later users can identify the material they received. NIST digital evidence preservation guidance

Match the same panel across the two sets

For example, your earliest image shows the left rear quarter panel at pickup. A later image after rain and storage shows a mark beside the wheel arch that is hard to see in the first shot. Match seams, trim or another fixed feature to confirm you are looking at the same spot. Note angle, lighting, wetness and any obstruction. The comparison is stronger when both views actually show the area being discussed.

If weather is part of the dispute, note the yard address and period outdoors. NOAA's Local Climatological Data can show precipitation observations for reporting stations. Record which station you used and how far it is from the lot. Station data provide context for the region and period. The condition of the car comes from its own records. NOAA Local Climatological Data

Keep condition and custody separate

Label each set by event, such as scene, pickup, first lot, transfer or inspection. Add the name of the person or business that provided it. If you can only establish that a mark was visible at the last inspection, state that directly and identify what earlier view would help. For records created when the car changed lots, use the tow intake and transfer guide. For photos intended to identify a specific safety component, read how to match the component to a recall record.
