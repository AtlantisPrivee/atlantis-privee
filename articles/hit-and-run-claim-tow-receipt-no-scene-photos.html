Filing a Hit-and-Run Claim With a Tow Receipt and No Scene Photos

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A tow receipt can put your car at a pickup location near the time you remember. The same ticket may record why the tow was requested or what the operator saw. Build the claim around the details the ticket actually contains, then look for independent records that speak to the collision itself.

Turn the tow ticket into a timeline

Copy the ticket number, VIN, pickup location, dispatch and pickup times, destination, and any damage notes. Clarify whether a printed time is when the operator arrived, when the vehicle was picked up or when the charge was entered. Ask the company for available dispatch or arrival records and original loading photos. Ask the receiving lot for its earliest images or condition record. Tow yard intake and transfer records guide

Use the police incident number to request the full available report and any supplement. If you remember the other vehicle, write down what you actually saw, including direction, color and any partial plate. Ask promptly about available footage from cameras pointed at the scene or the route away from it. Preserve witness contact details and record when you asked each source. Washington's insurance regulator gives guidance on reporting and working with the insurer after a crash with an uninsured or underinsured driver. Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner's guide

Separate a contact crash from a no-contact allegation

In Washington, the statutory definition of a phantom vehicle addresses crashes without physical contact. It requires corroboration from evidence other than testimony by the insured or a person with a resulting underinsured motorist claim, and reporting to the appropriate law-enforcement agency within 72 hours. The requirements for your state, policy and coverage may differ. Confirm the claim type and reporting requirements that apply to the facts you describe. Washington RCW 48.22.030(8)

A ticket showing your vehicle was towed from an intersection at 7:42 p.m. helps establish a place and approximate time. A dispatch call describing another vehicle leaving, a neutral witness's observation or footage can address a different part of the story. Give each record its own role instead of asking the tow ticket to establish the entire sequence.

Tell the insurer what the packet establishes

Use the Claim Evidence Organizer to group records under time, location, contact, identity and damage. Send a short note explaining that you have the tow ticket and identify the police, facility or camera records still pending. Ask how the carrier will review those records under the coverage you are claiming.

If the police narrative leaves out another contact, read how to ask about a crash-report supplement. For the broader reporting and coverage questions, see the hit-and-run claims guide.
