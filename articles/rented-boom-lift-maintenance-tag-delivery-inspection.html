The Rented Boom Lift's Maintenance Tag Predates Delivery

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A rented boom lift arrives on Monday with a service sticker dated Friday. A Tuesday incident raises a question about the lift's condition when it reached the site. Work backward from the serial number. The Friday sticker may document service at the rental yard. Delivery and daily checks describe later moments.

Separate service, handover and operation

Save the rental agreement and identify the make, model, serial number, hour meter and attachment configuration. Request the maintenance work order behind the sticker, including what was inspected, repaired or left open. Compare those records with the rental company's outbound condition sheet and the delivery ticket or receiving photos. Ask whether the same machine number appears on every document.

Collect the operator's pre-use or shift inspection record and any reported defect, swap or repair at the site. If a form says only that the equipment was received, record what it actually certifies. An operator's notes, delivery photos or the rental firm's service calls may provide separate evidence of the condition of a control, guard or other specific component.

Check which rule and procedure apply to the lift

For aerial lifts covered by OSHA's construction rule, lift controls must be tested each day before use. OSHA's aerial-lift guidance also describes pre-start inspection and following manufacturer recommendations. Look up the actual equipment type and applicable standard and compare the manufacturer checklist with what was recorded. A mechanic's service tag and a daily control test serve different purposes. OSHA construction aerial lift rule · OSHA aerial lift inspection guidance

Suppose a tag lists hydraulic service on Friday, the Monday ticket logs delivery at 7:42 a.m. and the Tuesday sheet marks “controls checked” without naming the machine. Ask which serial number the Tuesday check covered and whether it included the control involved. If a defect was first reported after delivery, date that report and identify who saw it.

Ask for records by serial number

Use the Records Request Letter Builder to name the work order, handover documents, daily inspection and defect communications. Keep the earliest photos with the time, position and source. If the lift was returned or repaired, ask what records document its condition at pickup and before the repair.
