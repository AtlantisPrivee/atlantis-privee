Two Revised EOBs Have the Same Claim Number. Which One Applies?

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

Two revised explanations of benefits, or EOBs, share the same claim number. One assigns $72 and the other assigns $49. Use the insurer's claim history to establish which version applies. The reference alone may identify the claim while leaving the sequence of decisions open.

Compare the notices as versions of the same record

Save both notices with their issue dates and any processing or revision dates shown. Record the patient, service dates, provider and line descriptions. Compare the amounts and explanation codes. Ask the insurer to confirm the status of each decision for those lines, whether it reversed or replaced an earlier decision and whether another adjustment is pending.

CMS's EOB guide explains how the claim number identifies the claim and where the patient amount appears. UnitedHealthcare's provider guide gives one example of a corrected claim that references the original. The insurer can tell you which identifiers or dates distinguish the specific versions on your account. CMS guide to EOB fields · UnitedHealthcare corrected claim guidance

Ask for the active amount for each line

For example, one notice is dated July 8 and the other July 12. The plan confirms the July 12 document is a copy of an older determination regenerated for delivery, while the July 8 decision is the active reprocessing result. Ask it to state the active amount and the date of that decision in writing. If each notice covers different lines, mark the active status on a line-by-line basis.

Ask the provider whether it received the same decision and when it posted it. Compare your payments and credits against that posting. Save the insurer's confirmation beside the copy you use for reconciliation so a later reviewer can follow your choice.

Ask which date refers to processing and which refers to printing or delivery if the notices use a single label. Note the name of the person who confirmed those dates. That gives you a basis to check another regenerated copy if the portal posts one later.

File both versions and mark the active one

Use the claim statement comparison tool to record what changed. If a provider submission triggered the revision, compare the original claim with the correction. If you find a reversal alongside the two versions, trace it through the remittance and provider's ledger.
