Requesting Crosswalk Signal Timing Records After a Pedestrian Crash

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

After a pedestrian crash, “send the traffic light timing” can get you a settings sheet for the entire intersection. That may tell you what the controller was configured to do. To examine what a particular crosswalk displayed, ask for the exact leg of the crossing and the records that tie an indication to the incident time.

Name the corner and the movement

Identify both streets, the side of the intersection, the pedestrian's direction and the vehicle movement involved. Describe the hour in local time with the date and a window that brackets the event. Include the agency's signal ID if available. A pedestrian walking east across the north leg and a driver turning right from a different approach may be governed by separate indications.

The federal MUTCD's traffic-signal provisions distinguish vehicle and pedestrian indications. The physical signal faces and phase assignments at the particular intersection determine which records you need to match. FHWA's current MUTCD edition and Part 4

Request the settings and the recorded events

Ask for the plan and phase diagram effective on the date, time-of-day schedules, pedestrian intervals for that crossing, and any logs showing controller events or unusual operation within your window. If the intersection uses a system with high-resolution logs, request the timestamps, phase or event codes and any data dictionary needed to read them. FHWA describes controllers that record timed inputs and outputs as part of automated traffic signal performance measurement systems. Confirm whether that capability was installed and whether the logs still exist. FHWA explanation of traffic signal performance data

A weekday timing sheet might show a seven-second walk setting. That number alone leaves open when the walk interval actually began during the relevant cycle, whether a push button placed a call and whether the controller was operating on the expected plan. Keep the plan, logs and observations distinct in your notes.

Ask the records office for existing documents

Please provide the active timing plan and phase diagram for the north-leg crosswalk at Oak Street and First Avenue on May 4, and any available controller event logs from 4:10 to 4:30 p.m. local time. Please include code definitions or related controller configuration records for interpreting that export.

Identify the road owner before sending the request. For example, New York City DOT's FOIL instructions ask for a specified intersection and date range and direct requesters to ask for records. Other agencies use their own channels. NYC DOT public records instructions

Use the Records Request Letter Builder to adapt the request. To compare the records with recorded images, see how to request a native video export. If the key witness saw a signal across the intersection, map which signal the witness actually observed.
