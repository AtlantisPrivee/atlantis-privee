You Sent a California Government Vehicle Claim to the Wrong Agency

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

After a crash with a government vehicle, you mailed the claim to the department on the door. The agency's insurer says another office handles claims. Identify the legal public entity and the statutory recipient. The department that operates a vehicle and the office authorized to receive a claim may be different.

Confirm which public entity the documents identify

Keep the crash report, agency vehicle identifiers, registration information if available and any letter naming the employer, operator or claims administrator. Ask the department to confirm the legal name of the entity responsible for receiving claims. A city, county and the State of California can have separate routes. California Courts' guide distinguishes the state claims process from filing with a local agency. It describes the usual six-month presentation deadline for injury or property damage claims. California Courts government claim guide

Trace where the first submission actually went

Keep the signed claim, envelopes or delivery records, and any acknowledgment from the office that received it. Ask if it forwarded the exact claim, to whom and when. Request the receiving office's proof of receipt. California Government Code § 915 sets out presentation methods. It also has an actual-receipt provision that can apply when the proper recipient receives the claim within the prescribed time. The listed recipient depends on the entity. California Government Code § 915

For example, a local department acknowledges a letter on Monday and sends it to the public entity's clerk on Thursday. Confirm when the clerk actually received the claim, the version that arrived and the deadline for the claim. The department's Monday acknowledgment and the clerk's receipt document different facts.

Address any remaining time while checking receipt

If the usual period is still open, confirm the proper form and presentation method for the entity promptly. If it may have expired, get advice about whether a late-claim application under § 911.4 or another route applies. That section has its own requirements and time limits. Keep a record of any rejection or late-claim notice and the date you received it. California Government Code § 911.4
