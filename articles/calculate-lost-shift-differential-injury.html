How to Calculate a Shift Differential Lost During an Injury Absence

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Before your injury, you were scheduled to work nights. When you returned, the employer assigned day shifts at the same base rate. To measure what changed, use the actual schedule and payroll rules for the night premium. Track full missed shifts separately from shifts you worked at a different rate.

Confirm which hours qualified for the premium

Collect the posted schedule, time cards, wage statements and the employer's pay policy or applicable agreement. Ask payroll to identify the premium amount, eligible hours and any conditions. Some policies apply a premium to certain hours within a shift. Others pay a different rate for the full shift. Match the applicable rule to each work date.

Compare the schedule made before the injury with what was worked or paid. If your shifts changed from week to week, use a representative set of actual schedules and explain why they are comparable. Ask the employer to confirm any known staffing or schedule change that would have affected the assignment regardless of your injury.

Calculate the premium and the base amounts separately

Suppose two eight-hour night shifts were assigned, the base rate was $22 and the differential was $2.50 per eligible hour. If all 16 hours qualified, the premium was $40 and the base pay was $352. If both shifts were unpaid because they were missed, those two components total $392 before any other payroll item. If you worked the same 16 hours on days at the base rate, the difference in this example is the $40 premium.

Overtime requires its own comparison. The federal overtime regulation includes shift differentials in the regular rate for overtime under the FLSA. Ask payroll for its actual calculation if overtime would have applied. Keep any paid leave or replacement payment in a separate column and identify what period it covers. 29 CFR § 778.207 on shift premiums and overtime

Send an itemization a reviewer can recalculate

Use the Hourly Lost Work Calculator for the base hours and an attached line for each shift premium or overtime item. The California civil instruction on past earnings illustrates the need to support the amount actually lost. Applicable damages rules depend on the case.
