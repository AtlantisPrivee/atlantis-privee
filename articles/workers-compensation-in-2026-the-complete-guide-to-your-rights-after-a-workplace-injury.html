Workers’ Compensation After an Injury: Claims, Care, and Pay

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 June 15, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Workers' compensation procedures depend on the employer, job, and jurisdiction. State programs handle most private-sector employment claims, while federal workers and some other groups may have separate programs. Start with the agency responsible for your employment and the employer's reporting process. U.S. Department of Labor directory of state workers’ compensation officials

Report the injury and keep proof of the claim

Obtain necessary care and report the injury through the employer's process promptly. Write down when and how you reported it, who received the report, and what form you were given. Ask where to file and how to keep proof of receipt. In California, the Division of Workers' Compensation describes a DWC 1 claim form and instructs workers to retain a copy. Follow the process that applies to your job and state. California DWC claim filing guide

For an injury that developed over time, document when symptoms affected work and when you reported or learned of a possible work connection. Keep the employer's and clinician's dates separate. Confirm any deadline with its source. Use the Insurance Claim Timeline Builder to organize the record.

Separate care, wage benefits, and restrictions

California's workers' compensation guide describes medical care, temporary disability payments for qualifying wage loss, and other benefits. A claims administrator and state rules may determine the treatment process, including provider selection. In California that can depend on predesignation and network arrangements. Ask which treatment and claim procedures apply before relying on a general rule. California DWC injured worker guidebook · California DWC medical care guidance

If you receive a return-to-work restriction, keep the dated note, proposed duties, actual hours, and pay stubs together. Temporary disability benefits and the wages you would have earned are different amounts. The Return-to-Work Hours and Pay Calculator compares schedule scenarios. The lost wages guide explains how to document actual pay changes. California DWC temporary disability guidance

Identify disputes and separate processes

If the claim is denied or treatment is disputed, ask for the written decision, reason, review process, and deadline. Identify the agency that handles the issue. Workplace safety or retaliation complaints can have separate procedures from a benefits dispute. OSHA explains how to report hazards and retaliation under the laws it administers. OSHA complaint guidance

When equipment or a different company's conduct contributed to an injury, a third-party claim may need separate review. The construction accident guide shows how to distinguish a workers' compensation file from a possible civil claim involving another party.
