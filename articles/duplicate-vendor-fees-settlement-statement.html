How to Spot a Vendor Fee Listed Twice on a Settlement Statement

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 10, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

A vendor appears under both case costs and a lien reduction on your settlement statement. Ask the firm to match each line to a document and a cash movement. A reduction changes the amount payable to a claimant, while a vendor invoice may represent a separate cost for work performed. A duplicate can arise when the same invoice is subtracted in both places.

Ask what each line represents

Find the vendor name, invoice number, service date, amount and person who paid. Ask whether the vendor gathered medical records, provided a negotiation service or did other work. Then find the lienholder's written payoff and any document that explains the reduction. Match those to the settlement statement.

The State Bar of California separates attorney fees from case expenses and recommends checking what the client pays directly or reimburses. In Medicare cases, federal rules describe how eligible procurement costs can affect the amount Medicare recovers. Those rules concern the recovery calculation. Ask the firm how it accounted for any invoice separately. California State Bar guide to case costs · Federal Medicare procurement cost rule

Test for a duplicate with the net calculation

Suppose a hypothetical original lien was $6,000, the written payoff is $4,500 and a vendor charged $300 under a separate agreement. The $1,500 reduction explains the change to the lien. The two outgoing amounts, if owed and paid from the settlement, are $4,500 to the lienholder and $300 to the vendor. Together they reduce the proceeds by $4,800. If the statement subtracts the same $300 again as a lien-reduction charge, ask the preparer to identify the second invoice or correct the arithmetic.

Sometimes similar charges have different invoice numbers or cover different work. Ask for both records and check whether one was already paid by the client or another party. A payee, reference and date make the distinction easier to verify than a category name.

Ask for an itemized correction if the invoice repeats

Use the Duplicate Settlement Deduction Checker to compare invoice IDs and overlapping entries. Then verify the result against the underlying paperwork. If the difference is a write-off, record it as a separate reduction alongside cash actually paid. If the same invoice was partly prepaid, trace the prepaid portion to its receipt.
