What an Injury-Claim Photo Can Show About Activity Duration

✍️ Atlantis Editorial 📅 November 7, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

A photo shows you standing on a trail after a crash. The picture captures a moment of standing. To describe the activity accurately, document how long you walked, how often you stopped, what help you used and what happened afterward. Start with the original file and keep your explanation separate from the image.

Distinguish capture time from activity time

Preserve the photo as received or taken, including the surrounding files if they are available. Record who captured it, when and how you obtained it. Save any original metadata and compare capture time, posting time and the time shown by a second record. If you make a marked-up version, identify it as a working copy. NIST's evidence guidance discusses keeping digital material intact and documenting its handling. NIST digital evidence preservation guidance

Build a short activity record. Use confirmed start and stop times where you have them, any stops or assistance, and the source for each detail. If you estimate a duration from a text or a witness's recollection, label it as an estimate. A sequence of photos can provide more context than an individual image, particularly when combined with a route, entry receipt or message.

Show the parts of the activity the photo leaves open

In a hypothetical example, a picture at 11:12 a.m. shows a person standing at an overlook. A parking receipt and a companion's messages place the outing between 10:58 and 11:30. The person walked for roughly six minutes, rested twice and accepted a ride back. Put the documented period, walking estimate and observed assistance in separate fields. If the symptoms changed later that afternoon, keep a contemporaneous note with its own time.

In Forman v. Henkin, New York's Court of Appeals addressed requests for social media material in an injury case and tied the scope of disclosure to relevance and the issues being litigated. The court discussed the kinds of information images and metadata could reveal. Review the scope of any actual discovery request with counsel and preserve the original material. Record the context while it is fresh

Use the Daily Function Journal to record duration, breaks, help and recovery time. Keep the note factual and tie it to the corresponding date.
